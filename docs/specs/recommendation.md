# Recommendation

## Definition

**Recommendation** is the capability through which Parallel You presents a possible interpretation, choice, Plan, Activity, revision, or response for the Person's consideration.

A Recommendation relates available evidence, Intentions, Commitments, Context, capacity, uncertainty, and anticipated consequences to something the Person may choose to consider or do.

A Recommendation is neither a command nor a decision made on the Person's behalf.

## Purpose

Recommendation allows Parallel You to:

- reduce the burden of identifying relevant choices;
- surface timely and suitable possible action;
- help the Person respond to changing conditions;
- expose alternatives, Trade-offs, assumptions, and uncertainty;
- connect present capacity with meaningful Activities;
- identify when clarification, Planning, Reflection, communication, rest, or non-action may be useful;
- and support judgment while preserving the Person's authority.

The purpose of Recommendation is not to maximize compliance, engagement, productivity, or system influence.

Its purpose is to make useful choices easier to see and evaluate.

## Scope

A Recommendation may concern:

- an Activity or next action;
- a Plan or Planning process;
- revision, suspension, or abandonment of a Plan;
- clarification of an Intention or Commitment;
- renegotiation, delegation, or release;
- scheduling or protection of Time;
- recovery, reserve, or reduced demand;
- Capture, Tracking, or Reflection;
- communication with another Party;
- selection among alternatives;
- deferral or deliberate non-action;
- or consultation with an appropriate human or external authority.

Not every relevant condition requires a Recommendation.

## Recommendation and the Person

The Person remains the authority over whether a Recommendation is relevant, acceptable, valuable, or acted upon.

The Person may:

- accept;
- reject;
- ignore;
- defer;
- modify;
- request alternatives;
- or ask why a Recommendation was made.

Rejection or non-response does not reduce the Person's authority and need not be justified.

Parallel You shall not interpret acceptance as permission to generalize the same choice across other Times or Contexts.

## Recommendation and Planning

Planning develops and evaluates possible courses of action.

Recommendation selects what Planning result, Activity, option, question, or revision may be useful to present.

Planning may produce several candidates or no Plan. Recommendation may present one, several, or none.

A Planning result shall not automatically become a Recommendation merely because it appears feasible or highly ranked.

Recommendation should consider timing, relevance, burden, confidence, and the Person's likely need for choice.

## Recommendation and Plan

A Recommendation may propose that the Person consider, accept, revise, replace, pause, or abandon a Plan.

A recommended Plan is not an accepted Plan.

The Recommendation should preserve material:

- assumptions;
- constraints;
- alternatives;
- uncertainty;
- anticipated capacity;
- and reasons for relevance.

Parallel You shall not present a Plan as inevitable merely because it is recommended.

## Recommendation and Activity

A Recommendation may propose an Activity suited to the Person's present or anticipated conditions.

Suitability may depend upon:

- relevant Intentions or Commitments;
- available Time;
- Energy and Attention;
- Context;
- resources;
- Activity demand;
- transitions;
- recovery;
- urgency;
- and what capacity should remain in reserve.

Recommending an Activity does not schedule, accept, begin, or observe it.

The system shall not recommend demanding Activity solely because the Person appears capable of performing it.

## Recommendation and Capture

Captured information may provide evidence for a Recommendation.

Recommendation may also propose clarification or further Capture when missing information materially affects choice, authority, risk, or feasibility.

An Expression shall not automatically be treated as a request for advice.

Parallel You should distinguish among:

- a request to be heard;
- a request for information;
- a request for options;
- a request for Planning;
- and a request for a Recommendation.

## Recommendation and Tracking

Tracking may identify current state, change, Staleness, gaps, thresholds, risk, or unresolved conditions relevant to Recommendation.

Detection does not require Recommendation.

Before recommending, Parallel You should consider:

- confidence in the tracked state;
- temporal and contextual scope;
- whether the Person already knows;
- interruption cost;
- consequence of delay;
- and whether a Recommendation would provide practical value.

Unknown State shall not be silently converted into a negative conclusion or urgent suggestion.

## Recommendation and Reflection

Reflection may produce understanding, questions, Insights, or Learning relevant to Recommendation.

Recommendation may propose Reflection when interpretation would be more useful than immediate action.

Reflection does not automatically require behavioural change.

Parallel You shall not turn every Insight, emotional expression, difficulty, or Pattern into advice.

Where the Person seeks understanding or witness, Recommendation may appropriately remain absent.

## Recommendation and Information

Providing information explains what is known, represented, or available.

Recommendation proposes what the Person may wish to consider.

The distinction may depend upon framing, selection, and Context.

Parallel You should not disguise a Recommendation as neutral information in order to avoid explaining its judgment.

Conversely, ordinary factual assistance shall not be burdened with recommendation machinery where no choice is being proposed.

## Recommendation and Decision

A Decision selects or adopts a course, interpretation, or commitment.

A Recommendation supports a Decision but does not constitute one unless an appropriately authorized agent has explicitly delegated decision authority.

Authority to recommend does not imply authority to decide.

Where decision authority has been delegated, Parallel You shall preserve:

- scope;
- criteria;
- limits;
- required confirmation;
- reversibility;
- and the Person's ability to inspect, override, or revoke delegation.

## Recommendation Subject

A **Recommendation Subject** is the choice, condition, concern, Plan, Activity, or question to which a Recommendation responds.

The Subject may be:

- explicitly requested by the Person;
- associated with an active Plan or Commitment;
- prompted by a relevant Event or state change;
- identified through Planning or Reflection;
- or suggested by Parallel You.

The Subject establishes scope.

Parallel You should not broaden a Recommendation into unrelated choices, sensitive Contexts, or life optimization without sufficient purpose and authority.

## Candidate

A **Recommendation Candidate** is an option considered for possible presentation.

A Candidate may be:

- an Activity;
- a Plan;
- an alternative or contingency;
- a clarification question;
- a communication;
- a review or Reflection;
- a change in scope, timing, or quality;
- deliberate rest or recovery;
- deferral;
- or non-action.

Consideration does not imply endorsement or presentation.

Candidates should preserve provenance where authorship or authority matters.

## Eligibility

**Eligibility** describes whether a Candidate satisfies the minimum conditions for consideration within the Recommendation Subject and Context.

Eligibility may depend upon:

- relevance to an Intention or Commitment;
- authority;
- feasibility;
- required resources;
- satisfied conditions;
- safety;
- privacy;
- and temporal applicability.

An eligible Candidate is not necessarily suitable, preferred, or worth presenting.

Unknown eligibility should remain distinguishable from ineligibility.

## Suitability

**Suitability** describes how well a Candidate appears to fit the Person's present or anticipated Context and capacity.

Suitability may consider:

- Time;
- Energy and Attention;
- Activity demand;
- reserve and recovery;
- location and environment;
- interruption risk;
- readiness;
- preferences;
- and the demands of other Intentions and Commitments.

Suitability is contextual, provisional, and Person-dependent.

Historical acceptance or success shall not establish universal suitability.

## Relevance

**Relevance** describes how meaningfully a Candidate bears upon the Recommendation Subject, the Person's Intentions or Commitments, and present conditions.

Relevance may be high without urgency.

An urgent condition may have no useful Recommendation.

Parallel You should avoid surfacing technically related options that do not materially assist the Person's present judgment.

The system shall not infer relevance solely from recency, frequency, measurable activity, or commercial availability.

## Timeliness

**Timeliness** describes whether the present moment is appropriate for making a Recommendation.

Timeliness may depend upon:

- decision windows;
- consequences of delay;
- current Energy and Attention;
- interruption cost;
- emotional Context;
- available information;
- reversibility;
- and whether the Person is able or willing to act.

A useful Recommendation delivered at the wrong Time may create burden rather than agency.

Parallel You may defer, summarize, or suppress a Recommendation when immediate presentation is not justified.

## Urgency

**Urgency** describes how quickly a relevant decision or response may be needed before consequences or opportunities materially change.

Urgency is distinct from importance, relevance, Commitment strength, and emotional intensity.

The system should preserve the source, consequence, and uncertainty of urgency.

A self-selected target shall not be represented as externally urgent.

Urgency shall not automatically override health, safety, authority, or the need for sufficient understanding.

## Importance

**Importance** describes the significance a Subject or Candidate has relative to the Person's Intentions, Commitments, values, responsibilities, or anticipated consequences.

Importance may be explicit, contextual, inferred, disputed, or unknown.

Parallel You shall not treat frequently tracked, externally requested, or easily measured matters as inherently more important.

Importance does not prove that immediate action is appropriate.

## Priority

Recommendation may use Priority to order attention among Subjects or Candidates.

Priority may consider:

- urgency;
- importance;
- consequence;
- Commitment strength;
- dependency;
- opportunity;
- feasibility;
- suitability;
- and reversibility.

These properties remain distinct.

Parallel You shall not allow a single opaque score to conceal why one Candidate was surfaced over another.

Priority is a presentation and decision aid, not a moral ranking.

## Criteria

**Recommendation Criteria** are properties used to evaluate, filter, compare, or explain Candidates.

Criteria may include:

- service to Intentions;
- satisfaction of Commitments;
- suitability;
- feasibility;
- sustainability;
- timeliness;
- expected benefit;
- burden;
- risk;
- flexibility;
- reversibility;
- and preservation of choice.

Criteria may be explicit, inferred, contextual, conflicting, or incomplete.

The Person's accepted Criteria shall take precedence over system defaults within applicable authority and safety constraints.

## Evidence

A Recommendation should be supported by evidence proportionate to its consequence and specificity.

Evidence may include:

- the Person's Expressions and decisions;
- current and historical states;
- Plans and Commitments;
- observed Activity and Outcomes;
- estimates;
- Reflection Insights;
- external information;
- and relevant Context.

Parallel You shall preserve meaningful provenance, uncertainty, Staleness, and contradiction.

Absence of evidence shall not silently become evidence of absence.

## Assumptions

A Recommendation may depend upon assumptions concerning the Person's state, preferences, authority, available resources, future conditions, or desired Outcome.

Material assumptions should be visible where they affect evaluation or acceptance.

An assumption shall not be presented as fact merely because it simplifies the Recommendation.

Where consequence is low and the Recommendation is reversible, the system may use provisional assumptions while making correction easy.

## Uncertainty

Uncertainty may concern:

- the Recommendation Subject;
- current state;
- the Person's Intention;
- Candidate feasibility or suitability;
- expected Outcomes;
- external conditions;
- and the Person's likely preference.

Recommendation may respond through:

- conditional language;
- alternatives;
- confidence;
- clarification;
- reversible suggestions;
- or withholding a Recommendation.

The system shall not manufacture confidence merely to sound helpful or decisive.

## Alternatives

Recommendation may present one or more alternatives.

Alternatives may differ in:

- scope;
- demand;
- timing;
- risk;
- reversibility;
- quality;
- cost;
- or the Intention or Commitment they emphasize.

The number of alternatives should be sufficient to preserve meaningful choice without transferring excessive comparison burden to the Person.

Parallel You shall not present trivial variants as meaningful choice while silently excluding materially different options.

## Ranking

**Ranking** orders Candidates according to applicable Criteria and evidence.

Ranking may be useful but is not always necessary.

A higher-ranked Candidate is not objectively best. It is preferred under a particular model, Context, and set of Criteria.

Parallel You should make the principal basis for consequential Ranking understandable.

Ranking shall not imply unsupported precision, especially where Criteria are qualitative, conflicting, or uncertain.

## Selection

**Selection** chooses which Candidates, if any, to present.

Selection should consider:

- relevance;
- timeliness;
- value;
- burden;
- confidence;
- novelty;
- existing awareness;
- and the Person's current capacity.

Selection is not acceptance by the Person.

The system may appropriately select no Candidate when presentation would not improve agency.

## Presentation

Recommendation Presentation communicates a Candidate and enough supporting information for the Person to understand and govern it.

Presentation may include:

- the proposed option;
- why it appears relevant;
- material assumptions;
- expected benefits and costs;
- uncertainty;
- alternatives;
- consequences of delay or non-action;
- and what authority or confirmation would be required.

Presentation depth should reflect consequence, complexity, urgency, and the Person's available Attention.

Parallel You should lead with the useful choice rather than burying it beneath its analysis.

## Explanation

The Person should be able to obtain an explanation proportionate to the Recommendation's consequence.

Explanation may identify:

- the Subject;
- relevant evidence;
- Criteria;
- assumptions;
- why the Candidate was selected;
- meaningful alternatives;
- uncertainty;
- and known limitations.

Explanation need not expose every internal operation.

It shall be sufficient to support informed acceptance, rejection, correction, and trust.

## Framing

Framing affects how a Recommendation is understood.

Parallel You should present Recommendations as options for consideration, with urgency or obligation only where supported.

The system shall not use:

- moral pressure;
- shame;
- manufactured scarcity;
- false consensus;
- exaggerated certainty;
- or emotional manipulation

to increase acceptance.

Language should distinguish obligation, advice, possibility, preference, prediction, and system uncertainty.

## Interruption

An unsolicited Recommendation may interrupt the Person's Activity, Attention, rest, or emotional process.

Before interrupting, Parallel You should consider:

- urgency;
- consequence;
- confidence;
- existing awareness;
- current Attention mode;
- recovery and health;
- and whether deferral would materially reduce value.

The technical ability to notify does not establish justification to interrupt.

Recommendations may be queued, summarized, bundled, or withheld.

## Recommendation Burden

**Recommendation Burden** is the Time, Energy, Attention, emotional effort, and decision-making imposed by receiving and evaluating a Recommendation.

Burden may arise through:

- excessive frequency;
- too many alternatives;
- repeated reminders;
- overlong explanation;
- unnecessary urgency;
- correction of poor assumptions;
- or pressure to respond.

Parallel You should account for cumulative Recommendation Burden.

A Recommendation should not require more effort to evaluate than the decision reasonably warrants.

## Frequency and Repetition

Recommendation Frequency should reflect value, consequence, uncertainty, Context, and the Person's preferences.

Repeated presentation may be appropriate when:

- material conditions changed;
- consequence increased;
- the Person requested reminders;
- or an accepted review condition applies.

Non-acceptance shall not automatically justify repetition.

Parallel You shall not nag, escalate tone, or manufacture urgency merely because a Recommendation was ignored or rejected.

## Non-Action

Deliberate non-action may be a valid Recommendation.

Non-action may preserve:

- recovery;
- optionality;
- privacy;
- resources;
- emotional space;
- or Time for uncertainty to resolve.

Parallel You shall not presume that every detected condition requires intervention.

Doing nothing, waiting, resting, observing, or declining further optimization may be appropriate courses.

## Recovery and Sustainability

Recommendation should consider whether a Candidate preserves or consumes recovery and long-term capacity.

A Candidate may be feasible while unsustainable.

Relevant factors may include:

- cumulative demand;
- Energy Reserve;
- Attention fragmentation;
- transition and recovery cost;
- illness or health Context;
- effects on later Commitments;
- and the Person's experienced cost.

The capacity to continue working shall not be treated as evidence that continued work is advisable.

Parallel You should be willing to recommend stopping, reducing scope, resting, or protecting reserve where supported and appropriate.

## Conflict and Trade-offs

A Recommendation may expose conflict among Intentions, Commitments, constraints, Parties, risks, or uses of capacity.

It may present Trade-offs without resolving them.

Where Criteria do not determine a single answer, Parallel You should preserve the value judgment for the Person.

The system shall not silently favour externally imposed, measurable, urgent, or productivity-oriented concerns over less visible parts of the Person's life.

## Risk

Recommendation should consider the risks of action, inaction, delay, interruption, and error.

Risk may involve likelihood, consequence, reversibility, detectability, and uncertainty.

Higher-consequence Recommendations may require:

- stronger evidence;
- explicit assumptions;
- clearer alternatives;
- authoritative sources;
- professional consultation;
- or explicit confirmation.

Parallel You shall not provide unsupported certainty in medical, legal, financial, safety-critical, or otherwise consequential matters.

## Reversibility

Reversibility affects how strongly a Recommendation may be made and what confirmation it requires.

Low-consequence, reversible Candidates may be presented provisionally with lighter evidence.

Irreversible, externally consequential, or difficult-to-recover actions require greater care and authority.

An action is not meaningfully reversible merely because a software control can be undone if people, obligations, privacy, or external systems have already been affected.

Parallel You should prefer reversible administrative actions where they adequately serve the Person.

## Acceptance

A Recommendation is **Accepted** when the Person or an appropriately authorized Party adopts it for present use.

Acceptance may be:

- explicit;
- partial;
- conditional;
- provisional;
- or delegated within a defined scope.

Viewing, discussing, editing, saving, or failing to reject a Recommendation does not necessarily constitute acceptance.

Acceptance shall not be generalized beyond the Recommendation's Subject, Context, conditions, or Time.

## Rejection and Deferral

The Person may reject or defer a Recommendation without explanation.

Rejection does not establish that:

- the Recommendation was irrational;
- the underlying Intention is invalid;
- the Person lacks Motivation or capacity;
- or a stronger Recommendation is required.

Deferral may reflect timing, uncertainty, insufficient capacity, competing concerns, or simple preference.

Parallel You should preserve rejection or deferral only to the degree useful for continuity and future burden reduction.

## Modification

The Person may modify a Recommendation before accepting it.

Modification may change:

- scope;
- timing;
- Activity;
- sequence;
- quality;
- conditions;
- delegation;
- or expected Outcome.

A modified Recommendation may become a new Plan, Activity, or Decision authored partly or wholly by the Person.

Parallel You should preserve authorship and material changes where they affect later interpretation.

## Execution and Delegation

Acceptance of a Recommendation does not necessarily authorize execution.

Execution may require separate authority to:

- schedule;
- communicate;
- purchase;
- disclose information;
- modify external systems;
- delegate to another Party;
- or perform an irreversible action.

Parallel You shall preserve the boundary among recommendation, decision, authorization, and execution.

Delegated execution shall remain bounded, inspectable, and revocable where appropriate.

## Outcome

A Recommendation may later be related to observed action, Events, and Outcomes.

The Person may:

- accept but not act;
- act without explicit acceptance;
- modify the Candidate;
- achieve an Outcome through another course;
- or experience consequences unrelated to the Recommendation.

Parallel You shall not claim causal credit or blame solely from temporal sequence or apparent adherence.

Recommendation quality should not be judged only by acceptance or immediate Outcome.

## Feedback

The Person may provide Feedback concerning relevance, timing, framing, accuracy, burden, or usefulness.

Feedback may be explicit or cautiously inferred from interaction.

Silence, rejection, and non-execution are ambiguous and shall not automatically be interpreted as negative Feedback.

Feedback may inform future Recommendation, but it shall not silently become a stable preference or universal rule.

The Person should be able to correct a Recommendation without being required to train the system extensively.

## Evaluation

Recommendation Evaluation examines whether a Recommendation was appropriately generated and presented under the information and authority available at the Time.

Evaluation may consider:

- relevance;
- evidence;
- uncertainty;
- suitability;
- timeliness;
- burden;
- explanation;
- alternatives;
- and effects on agency.

Acceptance rate, completion, engagement, and productivity are not sufficient measures of Recommendation quality.

A rejected Recommendation may have been appropriate; an accepted Recommendation may have been harmful or poorly supported.

## Learning

Parallel You may learn from Recommendation outcomes and Feedback.

Learning may revise:

- suitability estimates;
- timing;
- presentation;
- preferred level of detail;
- Candidate generation;
- or when not to recommend.

Learning shall remain contextual, provisional, and distinguishable by provenance.

Historical acceptance shall not become a mandate for future similarity.

The system shall not optimize Recommendations primarily to increase acceptance, engagement, or dependence.

## Automation

Parallel You may automate bounded Recommendation functions such as:

- Candidate retrieval;
- eligibility filtering;
- ranking;
- timing;
- summarization;
- and suppression of low-value repetition.

Automation shall preserve applicable Criteria, authority, uncertainty, and explanation.

The system shall not use opaque automation to evade responsibility for consequential selection or framing.

Automated Recommendation shall remain subordinate to the Person's agency and configurable boundaries.

## Privacy and Authority

Recommendation may combine sensitive information across health, relationships, work, location, obligations, preferences, and capacity.

Authority to Capture, Track, or access information does not automatically authorize its use for every Recommendation.

Parallel You should consider:

- purpose;
- Context;
- sensitivity;
- source expectations;
- audience;
- and whether the Recommendation reveals information the Person did not expect to be combined.

Information about another Party shall not be disclosed merely to explain or strengthen a Recommendation.

## External Influence

Recommendations may be affected by external Parties, institutions, commercial interests, platform objectives, or imported rankings.

Parallel You shall preserve and disclose material external influence where it could affect trust or choice.

The system shall not present sponsored, commercially preferred, institutionally convenient, or engagement-maximizing options as though selected solely for the Person's benefit.

The Person's welfare and agency shall not be subordinated to hidden optimization objectives.

## Failure and Withholding

Recommendation may fail to produce a useful Candidate because:

- the Subject is unclear;
- evidence is insufficient;
- uncertainty is too consequential;
- no Candidate is feasible or suitable;
- authority is absent;
- the Person's present need is not advice;
- or Recommendation Burden exceeds expected value.

Withholding a Recommendation may be the correct outcome.

Parallel You should state material uncertainty or inability without fabricating advice merely to appear useful.

## Error and Correction

A Recommendation may be affected by error in Capture, Tracking, Planning, Reflection, identity resolution, evidence, assumptions, criteria, or presentation.

Correction should:

- distinguish error from changed circumstances;
- identify affected decisions or actions where appropriate;
- withdraw or revise the Recommendation;
- preserve relevant provenance;
- and avoid shifting blame to the Person.

Correction should be timely and proportionate to consequence.

## Retention

A Recommendation may be retained with:

- its Subject;
- Candidate;
- Time and Context;
- supporting evidence and assumptions;
- acceptance state;
- material modifications;
- and known Outcomes or Feedback.

Not every transient Candidate or internal ranking requires durable retention.

Retention should reflect consequence, sensitivity, future value, explanation needs, and the Person's expectations.

Historical Recommendations shall not define what the Person should want in the future.

## Lifecycle

A Recommendation may be:

- generated;
- withheld;
- selected;
- presented;
- awaiting response;
- accepted;
- modified;
- deferred;
- rejected;
- withdrawn;
- superseded;
- expired;
- acted upon;
- or no longer relevant.

Not every Recommendation passes through every state.

More than one description may apply. A Recommendation may be accepted, modified, and awaiting separately authorized execution.

Lifecycle state shall preserve the distinction among presentation, acceptance, authorization, action, and Outcome.

## Relationships

Recommendation may:

- respond to a Person's request, Event, condition, Plan, Commitment, or tracked change;
- receive Candidates and reasoning from Planning;
- use captured, tracked, and reflected information;
- evaluate relevance, eligibility, suitability, timeliness, burden, risk, and uncertainty;
- select and present Activities, Plans, questions, alternatives, non-action, or other responses;
- receive acceptance, rejection, modification, deferral, and Feedback;
- inform but remain distinct from decisions and execution;
- and produce evidence for Reflection without claiming causal authority over Outcomes.

These relationships support judgment and practical agency. They do not grant the system authority to decide what the Person must value or do.

## Principles

- A Recommendation is an option for consideration, not a command or decision.
- The Person remains the authority over relevance, value, acceptance, and action.
- Recommendation is distinct from information, Planning, Plans, decisions, authorization, and execution.
- Detection, eligibility, feasibility, and ranking do not require presentation.
- Relevance, importance, urgency, priority, suitability, and acceptance remain distinguishable.
- Uncertainty, assumptions, alternatives, burden, and external influence should remain visible where material.
- Non-action, rest, recovery, deferral, and withholding are legitimate Recommendation outcomes.
- Recommendation should preserve capacity, sustainability, and future choice rather than maximizing activity.
- Explanation and presentation should be proportional to consequence and available Attention.
- Recommendation quality is not measured by acceptance, adherence, engagement, completion, or productivity alone.
- The system should learn when not to recommend.

## Invariants

Every implementation of Parallel You shall preserve the following invariants:

- A Recommendation shall not be treated as equivalent to information, a Plan, a Decision, acceptance, authorization, execution, or observed action.
- Generating, selecting, ranking, or presenting a Candidate shall not be represented as acceptance by the Person.
- Viewing, discussing, editing, saving, or failing to reject a Recommendation shall not automatically constitute acceptance.
- Acceptance of a Recommendation shall not automatically authorize scheduling, communication, disclosure, purchase, delegation, or execution.
- Rejection, deferral, silence, or non-execution shall not be represented as personal failure, lack of Motivation, invalid Intention, or justification for pressure.
- An Expression, emotional disclosure, tracked change, threshold crossing, Pattern, or Insight shall not automatically be treated as a request for advice.
- Detection of a relevant condition shall not automatically require Recommendation, Notification, interruption, or action.
- Recommendation shall not silently substitute the system's optimization objectives for the Person's values, preferences, Intentions, or judgment.
- Productivity, engagement, acceptance, adherence, completion, and increased Activity shall not be presumed universal Recommendation objectives.
- Feasibility shall not be treated as proof of suitability, sustainability, desirability, importance, urgency, or acceptance.
- The capacity to perform or continue an Activity shall not be treated as evidence that the Activity is advisable or sustainable.
- Unknown, stale, inferred, derived, and assumed information shall not be represented as confirmed Current State.
- Importance, urgency, relevance, priority, Commitment strength, and emotional intensity shall remain distinguishable.
- Ranking shall not imply objective superiority or unsupported precision.
- Meaningful alternatives shall not be silently excluded merely to simplify choice or increase acceptance.
- Framing shall not use shame, moral pressure, manufactured scarcity, false consensus, exaggerated certainty, or emotional manipulation.
- Non-action, rest, recovery, delay, and preservation of reserve shall remain legitimate Candidates.
- Repetition shall not escalate merely because a Recommendation was ignored, rejected, deferred, or not executed.
- Association between Recommendation, action, and Outcome shall not be represented as causation without sufficient evidence.
- Historical acceptance, rejection, or Feedback shall not become an immutable preference or mandate for future behaviour.
- Authority to Capture, Track, Reflect upon, or Plan with information shall not imply unrestricted authority to use it for Recommendation, disclose it, or combine it across Contexts.
- Commercial, institutional, platform, or engagement objectives shall not be concealed as Recommendations selected solely for the Person's benefit.
- Recommendation shall not impose greater Time, Energy, Attention, emotional, privacy, or decision burden than its expected value justifies.
- The Person shall retain authority to inspect, question, reject, defer, modify, ignore, or act outside every Recommendation.
