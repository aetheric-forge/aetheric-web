# Planning

## Definition

**Planning** is the capability through which Parallel You develops, examines, and revises possible courses of action in service of the Person's Intentions and Commitments under anticipated conditions.

Planning relates Activities, Time, Energy, Attention, resources, constraints, dependencies, uncertainty, alternatives, and expected Outcomes to produce or revise one or more Plans.

Planning is an ongoing process of provisional reasoning. It does not determine what the Person must do or what will occur.

## Purpose

Planning allows Parallel You to:

- translate direction and obligation into possible action;
- reduce the burden of arranging, sequencing, and remembering;
- test possible courses against capacity and constraints;
- expose conflict, infeasibility, uncertainty, and trade-offs;
- preserve alternatives and contingencies;
- coordinate Activities across Time and Context;
- adapt Plans as conditions and understanding change;
- and support the Person's judgment without replacing it.

The purpose of Planning is not to maximize occupancy, output, adherence, or efficiency.

Its purpose is to make purposeful action more understandable, achievable, resilient, and governable by the Person.

## Scope

Planning may concern:

- a single next action;
- an Activity or Task;
- part of a day or a longer Interval;
- an Intention or Commitment;
- a Project, transition, journey, or recurring responsibility;
- coordination among several Parties;
- a response to an Event or condition;
- preservation of recovery, reserve, or unallocated capacity;
- or a longer and deliberately incomplete course through uncertain circumstances.

Planning may create, compare, revise, suspend, replace, or end Plans.

Not every Intention, Commitment, Activity, or available Interval requires Planning.

## Planning and the Person

The Person remains the principal authority over the purposes, trade-offs, and Plans considered through Planning.

Parallel You may:

- retrieve relevant representations;
- identify missing or conflicting information;
- generate alternatives;
- estimate demand and feasibility;
- expose assumptions and consequences;
- preserve decisions;
- and perform bounded revisions under delegated authority.

The system shall not treat optimization criteria as substitutes for the Person's judgment.

The Person may prefer a Plan that is slower, less efficient, less measurable, more exploratory, or otherwise inconsistent with the system's default ranking.

## Planning and Plan

A Plan is a provisional representation of a possible course of action.

Planning is the capability through which such representations are developed, evaluated, negotiated, and revised.

A Planning process may produce:

- one Plan;
- several alternatives;
- a partial Plan;
- a determination that existing Plans remain adequate;
- a finding of infeasibility;
- a request for clarification;
- or no Plan.

The existence of Planning activity does not require the production or adoption of a Plan.

## Planning and Capture

Capture provides Expressions, Observations, constraints, Intentions, Commitments, Activities, and other information that may support Planning.

Planning may identify information that should be Captured or clarified.

Incomplete Capture does not always prevent useful Planning.

Parallel You may proceed with explicit assumptions, ranges, alternatives, or deferred decisions where consequence and uncertainty permit.

The system shall not invent precision merely to satisfy a Planning model.

## Planning and Tracking

Tracking provides evidence concerning current, historical, expected, derived, stale, and unknown states.

Planning may use tracked information to understand:

- current conditions;
- unresolved dependencies;
- available capacity;
- recurring obligations;
- relevant change;
- and the currentness of assumptions.

Tracking does not determine what should be planned.

Missing or stale tracked information may increase uncertainty without making Planning impossible.

## Planning and Reflection

Reflection develops understanding from experience and available evidence.

Planning may use Insights, Learning, corrections, and changed interpretations produced through Reflection.

Planning may also create review points at which Reflection would be useful.

Reflection does not automatically require a new Plan, and Planning shall not force every Insight into behavioural change.

The Person may understand an experience without choosing to optimize against it.

## Planning and Recommendation

Planning constructs and evaluates possible courses of action.

Recommendation determines what option, Plan, Activity, revision, or question may be useful to present for the Person's consideration.

Planning may provide candidates and supporting reasoning for Recommendation.

A Planning result shall not automatically be presented, endorsed, or treated as preferred merely because it was generated.

Parallel You may Plan without recommending and recommend further Planning rather than a particular Plan.

## Planning and Scheduling

Scheduling assigns or associates Activities, Events, Commitments, or protected purposes with Time.

Scheduling may be part of Planning, but Planning may also concern:

- what should remain unresolved;
- which Activities are possible;
- alternatives and contingencies;
- dependencies and conditions;
- Energy and Attention compatibility;
- reserve and recovery;
- scope and quality;
- or whether action is presently appropriate.

A Plan need not become a Schedule.

Parallel You shall not schedule every selected Activity merely because temporal space appears available.

## Planning and Optimization

Optimization selects or improves a representation according to defined criteria.

Planning may use optimization, but Planning is not reducible to optimization.

Relevant criteria may be multiple, uncertain, conflicting, qualitative, and controlled by the Person.

Examples include:

- feasibility;
- sustainability;
- urgency;
- importance;
- reversibility;
- flexibility;
- enjoyment;
- learning;
- fairness;
- quality;
- risk;
- and preservation of future choice.

Parallel You shall not silently optimize for productivity, completion, utilization, speed, or adherence.

An optimal result under an incomplete objective is not necessarily a good Plan.

## Planning Subject

A **Planning Subject** is the Intention, Commitment, question, decision, period, Event, or concern around which Planning is organized.

The Subject establishes what the Planning process is attempting to serve or resolve.

A Planning Subject may be:

- explicitly selected by the Person;
- supplied by an authorized Party;
- derived from an accepted Commitment;
- prompted by an Event or condition;
- or suggested by Parallel You.

The system should not broaden the Subject beyond its useful and authorized scope without sufficient reason.

## Planning Horizon

A **Planning Horizon** is the future span for which Planning attempts to provide useful structure.

The Horizon may be:

- immediate;
- bounded by an Event or Deadline;
- fixed;
- rolling;
- conditional;
- approximate;
- or deliberately open.

Planning detail and certainty should generally decrease as the Horizon extends and uncertainty compounds.

Distant action may remain coarse, alternative, or condition-dependent until additional detail becomes valuable.

Parallel You shall not fill a long Horizon with false precision merely to make the Plan appear complete.

## Inputs

Planning Inputs may include:

- Intentions and Commitments;
- existing Plans and Recommendations;
- possible Activities and expected Outcomes;
- Time, Energy, Attention, and resources;
- Context and environment;
- constraints, conditions, and dependencies;
- Events and recurrence;
- current, historical, expected, and derived states;
- preferences, values, and accepted criteria;
- Insights and Learning;
- assumptions, estimates, and uncertainty;
- and authority concerning decisions or external action.

No Input is universally required.

The meaning, provenance, currentness, and confidence of Inputs may affect their appropriate use.

## Planning Context

Planning occurs within a Context that may determine which Inputs, constraints, resources, Parties, and consequences are relevant.

Contexts may overlap.

An Activity suitable in one Context may be unsuitable or unavailable in another.

An Intention, resource, or reserve associated with one Context shall not automatically be allocated to another.

Parallel You should preserve boundaries involving role, privacy, authority, and purpose where Planning spans several Contexts.

## Criteria

**Planning Criteria** are properties by which possible Plans or Activities may be evaluated.

Criteria may include:

- service to Intentions;
- satisfaction of Commitments;
- feasibility;
- sustainability;
- required quality;
- timeliness;
- risk;
- flexibility;
- reversibility;
- burden;
- uncertainty;
- and preservation of reserve.

Criteria may be explicit, inferred, provisional, contextual, conflicting, or unknown.

Parallel You shall not collapse distinct Criteria into a single universal priority or score where doing so conceals meaningful trade-offs.

## Requirements and Preferences

Planning should distinguish Requirements from Preferences.

A Requirement defines a condition necessary for a Plan, Activity, Outcome, obligation, or authorized action.

A Preference describes a condition that is favoured without making alternatives invalid.

Requirements and Preferences may concern Time, quality, sequence, location, tools, people, privacy, Energy, Attention, scope, or mode of action.

Parallel You shall preserve their source, scope, flexibility, and certainty.

A Preference shall not be hardened into a Requirement merely because earlier Plans accommodated it.

## Constraints

A **Planning Constraint** limits the set of possible Plans or how they may be carried out.

Constraints may be:

- physical;
- temporal;
- relational;
- institutional;
- legal;
- financial;
- informational;
- environmental;
- or imposed by capacity and health.

A Constraint may be hard, flexible, conditional, disputed, inferred, or unknown.

Planning should not bypass a Constraint through an unsupported assumption.

Where a Constraint conflicts with an Intention or Commitment, the conflict should remain visible.

## Assumptions

Planning depends upon assumptions about future conditions and incomplete information.

Assumptions may concern:

- availability;
- duration and demand;
- Energy and Attention;
- external cooperation;
- resources;
- sequence and dependencies;
- recurrence;
- expected Outcomes;
- or continued relevance.

Material Assumptions should be represented explicitly enough to support review and revision.

An Assumption shall not silently become fact through repeated use.

Planning should consider how the Plan changes if a consequential Assumption is false.

## Estimates

An **Estimate** is a provisional quantitative or qualitative representation used to support Planning.

Estimates may concern:

- Duration;
- Energy or Attention demand;
- available capacity;
- cost;
- probability;
- progress;
- effort;
- or expected Outcome.

Estimates should preserve range, confidence, source, conditions, and relevant historical evidence where useful.

Precision shall not exceed supporting evidence.

An Estimate from one Activity Occurrence or Context shall not automatically define another.

## Uncertainty

Planning necessarily concerns an uncertain future.

Uncertainty may concern:

- Inputs;
- assumptions;
- estimates;
- dependencies;
- external Events;
- capacity;
- acceptance;
- and Outcomes.

Planning may respond through ranges, alternatives, contingencies, reserve, delayed decisions, monitoring conditions, or explicit unknowns.

Uncertainty shall not be concealed merely to produce a definite-looking Plan.

A Plan may be useful while remaining materially uncertain.

## Candidate Activities

Planning may identify Activities that could serve an Intention or Commitment.

Candidate Activities may be:

- provided by the Person;
- retrieved from existing representations;
- generated by Parallel You;
- imported from another source;
- derived from a prior Plan;
- or suggested by another Party.

A Candidate Activity is not selected, accepted, scheduled, or undertaken merely because it was considered.

Parallel You should preserve provenance and avoid narrowing the space of possible action to what the system already knows how to represent.

## Decomposition

Planning may decompose broad Activities or Outcomes into more specific Activities.

Decomposition may improve:

- understanding;
- estimation;
- coordination;
- execution;
- delegation;
- or identification of a useful next action.

Decomposition also creates maintenance and cognitive burden.

Parallel You should decompose only to the level that improves practical agency.

The system shall not convert every broad Activity into an exhaustive hierarchy of Tasks.

## Composition

Planning may compose several Activities into a larger course of action.

Composition may identify:

- shared purpose;
- sequence;
- concurrency;
- common Context;
- shared resources;
- cumulative demand;
- transitions;
- and combined Outcomes.

Composition shall not imply that every Activity must remain in the Plan or that the Plan is complete only when every component is complete.

An Activity may serve several Plans without being duplicated as action.

## Dependencies and Ordering

Planning may represent Dependencies and temporal Ordering among Activities, Events, conditions, resources, and Outcomes.

A Dependency expresses that one element requires or relies upon another.

Ordering expresses temporal sequence.

Temporal precedence alone shall not establish Dependency.

Dependencies may be confirmed, inferred, conditional, disputed, or unknown.

False Dependencies can make Plans unnecessarily rigid; missing Dependencies can make them infeasible.

## Alternatives

Planning may generate and preserve alternative Plans, Activities, sequences, scopes, or Outcomes.

Alternatives may be:

- mutually exclusive;
- conditionally selected;
- partially compatible;
- ranked;
- equivalent for present purposes;
- or deliberately unresolved.

Meaningful alternatives should remain visible where they preserve choice or expose trade-offs.

Parallel You shall not force convergence on a single option merely because presenting one Plan is simpler.

## Contingencies

Planning may define Contingencies for relevant uncertainty, Events, delays, failures, opportunities, or changes in capacity.

A Contingency may:

- substitute an Activity;
- change scope or quality;
- alter sequence or timing;
- draw upon reserve;
- trigger review;
- initiate communication or renegotiation;
- or end the Plan.

Not every possibility justifies preparation.

Contingency detail should remain proportional to likelihood, consequence, reversibility, and Planning burden.

## Feasibility

**Planning Feasibility** describes whether available evidence supports that a candidate Plan may be carried out under anticipated conditions.

Feasibility may consider:

- Time;
- Energy and Attention;
- resources;
- constraints;
- dependencies;
- external cooperation;
- transitions;
- recovery;
- reserve;
- and uncertainty.

Feasibility is contextual, provisional, and may be partial.

A feasible Plan is not necessarily desirable, sustainable, accepted, or worth its cost.

Planning shall not manufacture feasibility by ignoring ordinary life or assigning the same capacity more than once.

## Sustainability

**Planning Sustainability** concerns whether a Plan can be carried out without unacceptable or unrepresented cumulative cost to the Person's capacity and life.

Sustainability may consider:

- repeated demand;
- depletion and recovery;
- Attention fragmentation;
- transition cost;
- loss of reserve;
- effects on later Activities and Commitments;
- health and Context;
- and how long the Plan is expected to continue.

The ability to complete a Plan once shall not establish that it is sustainable.

Parallel You should expose sustainability concerns without claiming authority to determine which costs the Person must accept.

## Capacity

Planning should model capacity as finite, variable, contextual, and Activity-dependent.

Capacity may include:

- Time;
- physical, cognitive, emotional, social, executive, or creative Energy;
- Attention modes and continuity;
- resources;
- environmental suitability;
- and the ability to absorb uncertainty or interruption.

Available Time shall not be treated as proof of usable capacity.

Available Energy or Attention shall not automatically be treated as expendable capacity.

Capacity should not be allocated with greater precision than the model supports.

## Reserve

Planning may preserve **Reserve** for recovery, uncertainty, essential responsibilities, interruption, opportunity, and ordinary life.

Reserve may involve Time, Energy, Attention, resources, or flexibility.

Reserve is not unused capacity awaiting more valuable work.

Parallel You should avoid Plans that consume all estimated capacity merely because doing so appears feasible.

The Person may choose to spend Reserve, but the system shall not silently allocate it.

## Recovery

Planning should represent recovery where Activities, transitions, illness, stress, or sustained demand make it relevant.

Recovery may occur through rest, restorative Activity, environmental change, reduced demand, sleep, unstructured Time, or Person-specific conditions.

Recovery is not solely preparation for later productivity.

Parallel You shall not compress or remove recovery merely to fit additional Activities into a Plan.

The demand of an Activity may extend beyond its observed Duration through delayed recovery cost.

## Transition and Switching

Planning should consider the cost of moving between Activities, Contexts, environments, tools, roles, or Attention modes.

Transition may require:

- physical movement;
- preparation or cleanup;
- orientation;
- emotional adjustment;
- Attention disengagement;
- or recovery from residue.

Transitions occupy legitimate Time and capacity.

Parallel You shall not schedule Activities as though boundaries were frictionless.

## Buffers

A **Buffer** is capacity intentionally left between or around planned elements to absorb uncertainty, transition, variation, or recovery.

Buffers may concern Time, Energy, Attention, resources, or scope.

A Buffer is part of the Plan even when no conventional Activity occupies it.

Parallel You shall not treat Buffers as waste or automatically reclaim them when another Activity could fit.

Buffer size should reflect uncertainty, consequence, and the Person's tolerance for compression.

## Allocation

Planning may allocate Time, Energy, Attention, resources, or responsibility to an Activity, Intention, Commitment, Context, or purpose.

Allocation may be:

- fixed;
- flexible;
- tentative;
- conditional;
- reserved;
- shared;
- or unresolved.

Allocation does not create capacity.

Overlapping Allocations may represent conflict, concurrency, alternatives, imprecision, or several purposes served by one Activity.

Parallel You shall examine rather than automatically accept or reject overlap.

## Scheduling

Scheduling may assign Activities, Events, protected purposes, or Buffers to temporal Instants or Intervals.

A scheduled element should preserve relevant:

- flexibility;
- authority;
- confidence;
- duration uncertainty;
- transition;
- and applicable Time zone or calendar rules.

Planning should distinguish fixed appointments, flexible work, tentative reservations, protected Time, and conditional alternatives.

A Schedule is an expectation, not an observation or command.

## Concurrency

Planning may represent Activities or purposes that overlap in Time.

Concurrency may be feasible when Activities:

- require different forms of capacity;
- involve waiting;
- permit shared Attention;
- or are served by one action.

Apparent temporal overlap does not multiply Energy or Attention.

Parallel You shall not presume that overlapping Activities receive equal, continuous, or effective Attention.

Concurrency should include transition, interruption, safety, and quality effects where relevant.

## Recurrence

Planning may define or accommodate recurring Activities, Commitments, Events, review points, and recovery.

A recurrence definition is distinct from each expected Occurrence.

Planning should consider:

- variation among Occurrences;
- exceptions;
- holidays and calendar rules;
- cumulative demand;
- changes to future recurrence;
- and conditions for review or cessation.

A missed Occurrence shall not automatically be rescheduled or converted into accumulating debt.

## Prioritization

**Prioritization** helps determine which Subjects, Activities, Commitments, risks, or decisions should receive consideration before others.

Prioritization may consider:

- importance;
- urgency;
- Commitment strength;
- consequence;
- feasibility;
- readiness;
- dependency;
- opportunity;
- reversibility;
- and capacity compatibility.

These properties are distinct.

Parallel You shall not collapse them into a single universal priority score where meaningful differences would be lost.

Priority does not prove that an Activity is presently suitable or that lower-priority life should be displaced indefinitely.

## Trade-offs

Planning may expose Trade-offs among competing benefits, costs, risks, Intentions, Commitments, and uses of capacity.

A Trade-off may involve:

- speed and quality;
- effort and scope;
- certainty and flexibility;
- present action and future reserve;
- one Context or Commitment and another;
- or achievement and sustainability.

Parallel You should make material Trade-offs understandable without pretending they can always be resolved through calculation.

The Person remains the authority over value judgments not established by accepted criteria or obligation.

## Conflict

Planning may reveal conflict among Intentions, Commitments, Activities, constraints, Parties, or uses of capacity.

Conflict may be temporal, physical, relational, ethical, legal, informational, or contextual.

The existence of conflict does not automatically invalidate either side.

Possible responses may include:

- selection;
- sequencing;
- reduced scope;
- delegation;
- renegotiation;
- delay;
- clarification;
- or acknowledgement that no satisfactory Plan is presently available.

Parallel You shall not silently resolve conflict by discarding an Intention or Commitment.

## Coordination

Planning may coordinate action with other Persons, Parties, organizations, agents, or systems.

Coordination may require representing:

- roles;
- authority;
- shared constraints;
- availability;
- dependencies;
- communication;
- expected contributions;
- and differing understandings.

The system's representation of a shared Plan does not establish that every Party accepts it.

Information about one Party shall not be disclosed to another merely because it would simplify coordination.

## Delegation

Planning may identify Activities or responsibilities that could be delegated.

Delegation may alter demand, authority, coordination, risk, and required oversight.

Delegation of performance does not necessarily transfer the underlying Commitment.

Parallel You shall preserve:

- who delegated;
- to whom;
- what authority or Activity was delegated;
- applicable conditions;
- and how delegation may be inspected, revised, or revoked.

A Plan proposing delegation does not itself authorize external action.

## Risk

**Planning Risk** is the possibility that uncertainty, action, inaction, or changed conditions produce consequential harm or loss.

Risk may concern:

- health;
- safety;
- obligations;
- relationships;
- privacy;
- resources;
- opportunity;
- reputation;
- or system operation.

Risk includes likelihood, consequence, reversibility, detectability, and the Person's tolerance where known.

Parallel You shall not use unsupported numerical precision to disguise uncertain risk judgment.

Risk reduction is one criterion among others and shall not silently eliminate all experimentation or choice.

## Reversibility

**Reversibility** describes how easily a Planning decision or resulting action can be changed, undone, or recovered from.

Reversible choices may justify lighter evidence, reduced clarification, or provisional action within delegated authority.

Irreversible or difficult-to-reverse choices may require greater certainty, explicit acceptance, and careful review.

Reversibility depends upon Context and consequence, not merely whether software exposes an undo function.

Parallel You should prefer reversible administrative steps where they adequately serve the Plan.

## Planning Depth

**Planning Depth** describes how much analysis, detail, comparison, and future structure is introduced.

Greater depth may be useful when:

- consequences are high;
- dependencies are complex;
- coordination is required;
- uncertainty can be reduced;
- or future decisions depend upon present structure.

Greater depth also consumes Time, Energy, and Attention and can make Plans brittle.

Parallel You should use the least Planning Depth sufficient to support the Person's present judgment and action.

## Progressive Planning

**Progressive Planning** develops detail as relevance, information, and the need for action increase.

A Plan may begin with:

- a direction;
- a rough sequence;
- a protected Interval;
- a next action;
- a set of alternatives;
- or a review condition.

Later Planning may refine only the portion that has become actionable.

Progressive Planning should preserve coherence without requiring distant uncertain action to be specified prematurely.

## Planning Burden

**Planning Burden** is the Time, Energy, Attention, emotional effort, and administrative work required to develop and maintain Plans.

Burden may arise through:

- excessive decomposition;
- repeated rescheduling;
- too many alternatives;
- constant estimate refinement;
- unnecessary confirmation;
- rigid schemas;
- or Plans that require more maintenance than action.

Parallel You should reduce burden through defaults, reuse, progressive detail, selective clarification, and bounded automation.

Planning shall not become a substitute Activity that indefinitely delays action or rest.

## Clarification

Planning may request clarification when missing or ambiguous information materially affects feasibility, authority, risk, obligation, or meaningful choice.

Clarification should be:

- scoped to the current Planning need;
- proportionate to consequence;
- sensitive to the Person's capacity;
- and deferred where assumptions or alternatives are sufficient.

Parallel You shall not require the Person to resolve every uncertainty before Planning can continue.

Where the system proceeds without clarification, material assumptions should remain visible.

## Participation and Collaboration

Planning may be performed:

- by the Person;
- jointly with Parallel You;
- by Parallel You under delegated authority;
- with other authorized Parties;
- or through imported Plans and external systems.

Authorship, participation, and acceptance are distinct.

The Person may delegate construction or revision of a Plan without delegating the value judgments or external actions it contains.

Parallel You should preserve which decisions were made by whom where authority or later Reflection depends upon it.

## Acceptance

Planning may produce a Plan for the Person or another authorized Party to accept.

Acceptance may be explicit, partial, conditional, provisional, delegated, or unknown.

Viewing, discussing, editing, or failing to reject a Plan does not necessarily constitute acceptance.

Acceptance of a Plan does not mean:

- every Activity is mandatory;
- every assumption is confirmed;
- every automated action is authorized;
- or future revision is prohibited.

The Person may withdraw acceptance or request replanning.

## Revision

Planning may revise a Plan when:

- conditions change;
- an assumption proves inaccurate;
- capacity differs from expectation;
- an Event occurs;
- a dependency changes;
- Reflection produces new understanding;
- the Person changes direction;
- or the Plan no longer provides useful guidance.

Revision may alter scope, Activities, sequence, timing, allocations, assumptions, alternatives, contingencies, or Outcomes.

Revision is a normal response to reality, not evidence that Planning failed.

Relevant earlier Plan states should remain available rather than being silently overwritten.

## Replanning

**Replanning** reconsiders part or all of an existing Plan.

Replanning may preserve the underlying Intention or Commitment while changing the proposed means.

Repeated Replanning may indicate:

- ordinary adaptation;
- unstable conditions;
- inaccurate estimates;
- excessive Planning detail;
- an unresolved dependency;
- infeasibility;
- or a need to reconsider the Intention or Commitment.

Parallel You shall not use indefinite Replanning to conceal that no feasible Plan presently exists.

## Review

Planning may establish Review conditions based upon Time, Events, uncertainty, Progress, risk, or material change.

Review may determine whether to:

- continue;
- revise;
- deepen or simplify;
- select an alternative;
- renegotiate a Commitment;
- suspend;
- or end a Plan.

Review frequency should remain proportional to volatility, consequence, and burden.

The existence of tracked data does not require continuous Plan review.

## Failure and Infeasibility

Planning may fail to produce a useful Plan because:

- no feasible course is known;
- required information is unavailable;
- constraints conflict;
- capacity is insufficient;
- authority is unclear;
- risk is unacceptable;
- or the Subject is not yet ready for Planning.

An honest finding of infeasibility may be a successful Planning outcome.

Parallel You should expose the limiting factors and possible governance responses without blaming the Person.

The system shall not fabricate a Plan merely to avoid returning uncertainty or conflict.

## Error and Correction

Planning may contain errors in Inputs, assumptions, estimates, constraints, resolution, dependencies, calculation, or interpretation.

Correction should:

- distinguish error from changed conditions;
- preserve relevant provenance;
- identify affected Plans, Recommendations, and delegated actions;
- and support revision proportionate to consequence.

Correcting a Plan shall not rewrite original Sources or observed History.

Parallel You should make correction easier than reconstructing a Plan where practical.

## Automation

Parallel You may automate bounded parts of Planning, such as:

- retrieving relevant Inputs;
- estimating Duration or demand;
- generating candidates;
- detecting conflicts;
- arranging flexible Activities;
- preserving Buffers;
- or revising reversible Allocations.

Automation shall remain within explicit or appropriately established delegation.

Authority to construct or revise a Plan does not necessarily authorize scheduling, communication, purchasing, delegation to others, or execution.

Material automated decisions should remain inspectable and reversible where appropriate.

## Privacy and Authority

Planning may combine sensitive information across Contexts, sources, relationships, health, obligations, and capacity.

Parallel You should use only the information and authority justified by the Planning purpose.

Authority to access information does not automatically authorize:

- cross-context combination;
- disclosure to another Party;
- inference of sensitive properties;
- external coordination;
- or durable retention of Planning reasoning.

The Person shall retain appropriate authority to inspect, restrict, revise, or reject Planning and its Inputs.

## Retention

Planning may retain:

- Plans and relevant versions;
- assumptions and estimates;
- alternatives considered;
- accepted decisions;
- unresolved questions;
- and reasoning needed for trust, revision, or Reflection.

Not every generated candidate or discarded possibility requires durable retention.

Retention should reflect sensitivity, future value, audit needs, and burden.

Parallel You should preserve enough history to explain consequential Plans without creating an exhaustive archive of private deliberation by default.

## Lifecycle

A Planning process may be:

- initiated;
- scoped;
- gathering Inputs;
- generating candidates;
- evaluating;
- awaiting clarification;
- awaiting acceptance;
- active;
- revising;
- paused;
- completed;
- abandoned;
- blocked;
- or found infeasible.

Not every Planning process passes through every state or produces a Plan.

Planning state is distinct from the lifecycle state of any Plan it produces.

Lifecycle representations shall preserve uncertainty, authority, and relevant provenance.

## Relationships

Planning may:

- serve the Person's Intentions and Commitments;
- receive Inputs through Capture, Tracking, and Reflection;
- consider Activities, Events, Outcomes, Time, Energy, Attention, resources, constraints, and Context;
- generate and evaluate Plans, alternatives, contingencies, estimates, and allocations;
- expose conflict, Trade-offs, risk, infeasibility, and uncertainty;
- produce candidates for Recommendation;
- establish Scheduling, Tracking, or Review conditions;
- revise Plans under changed understanding or circumstances;
- and support delegated action without itself authorizing execution.

These relationships support practical agency. They do not grant Planning authority over the Person's purposes or choices.

## Principles

- Planning develops provisional courses of action in service of the Person's Intentions and Commitments.
- Planning is distinct from Plans, Scheduling, Recommendation, execution, and optimization.
- The Person remains the principal authority over purposes, criteria, Trade-offs, and acceptance.
- Incomplete information may be handled through explicit uncertainty, assumptions, ranges, alternatives, reserve, and progressive detail.
- Feasibility, sustainability, desirability, priority, and acceptance remain distinguishable.
- Time, Energy, Attention, recovery, transition, reserve, and ordinary life are legitimate Planning concerns.
- Alternatives and honest infeasibility are valid Planning outcomes.
- Revision and Replanning are ordinary responses to change, not evidence of failure.
- Planning Depth and burden should remain proportional to consequence and practical value.
- Planning should preserve future choice rather than consume all apparent capacity.

## Invariants

Every implementation of Parallel You shall preserve the following invariants:

- Planning shall not be treated as equivalent to a Plan, Schedule, Recommendation, prediction, execution, or record of what occurred.
- The production of a Plan shall not be represented as acceptance by the Person or authorization to execute it.
- Planning criteria shall not silently substitute the system's optimization goals for the Person's judgment.
- Productivity, utilization, speed, completion, and adherence shall not be presumed universal Planning objectives.
- An Intention, Commitment, Activity, or available Interval shall not be presumed to require Planning.
- Incomplete, stale, or unknown Inputs shall not be silently replaced with invented precision or certainty.
- Assumptions, Estimates, inferred constraints, and derived states shall remain distinguishable from confirmed facts.
- Requirements and Preferences shall remain distinguishable, and Preferences shall not be silently hardened into Requirements.
- Temporal Ordering shall not, by itself, establish Dependency.
- A Candidate Activity or Plan shall not be represented as selected, accepted, scheduled, or undertaken merely because it was considered.
- Meaningful alternatives shall not be silently discarded merely to produce a single apparently decisive Plan.
- Available Time shall not be treated as proof of available Energy, Attention, resources, or suitable capacity.
- Available capacity and Reserve shall not be presumed fully expendable or allocable.
- Feasibility shall not be manufactured by ignoring transition, recovery, Buffers, uncertainty, ordinary life, or conflicting Allocations and Commitments.
- The ability to complete a Plan once shall not be treated as proof that the Plan is sustainable.
- Buffers, Reserve, recovery, transition, and unallocated capacity shall not be treated as waste.
- Missed recurring Occurrences shall not automatically be rescheduled or converted into accumulating debt.
- Priority shall not be treated as proof that an Activity is presently suitable or that lower-priority life may be displaced indefinitely.
- Planning conflict shall not be silently resolved by discarding or rewriting an Intention or Commitment.
- Replanning shall not be used indefinitely to conceal infeasibility, insufficient capacity, or the need for renegotiation.
- Planning shall not impose greater Time, Energy, Attention, emotional, or administrative burden than its expected value justifies.
- Authority to construct or revise a Plan shall not imply authority to schedule, disclose, communicate, delegate, purchase, or execute.
- The Person shall retain authority to inspect, revise, reject, abandon, or replace Planning results and to choose action outside them.
