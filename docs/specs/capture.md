# Capture

## Definition

**Capture** is the capability through which Parallel You receives, preserves, and provisionally interprets information that may be relevant to the Person.

Capture may transform human expression, observations, imported records, Events, or other available evidence into representations that the system can retain and use.

Capture does not require the information to be complete, precise, actionable, or immediately classified.

The result of Capture is a representation of available information. It is not proof that the representation is complete, correct, accepted, or understood in the same way by the Person.

## Purpose

Capture allows Parallel You to:

- reduce the burden of remembering;
- preserve information before it is lost;
- support incomplete or low-effort expression;
- establish provenance and temporal context;
- distinguish what was expressed, observed, imported, inferred, and assumed;
- identify information that may merit clarification or later attention;
- create or update operational representations;
- and provide reliable inputs to Planning, Recommendation, Reflection, and Tracking.

The purpose of Capture is not to demand that the Person fully organize experience at the moment it occurs.

Its purpose is to preserve meaning with the least burden reasonably necessary.

## Scope

Capture may concern:

- Persons and Users;
- Intentions and Commitments;
- Activities, Events, and Outcomes;
- Plans and Recommendations;
- Time, Energy, and Attention;
- preferences, values, Contexts, and relationships;
- resources, constraints, conditions, and dependencies;
- decisions, questions, ideas, and uncertainties;
- external records;
- and information not yet understood well enough to classify.

Capture may create a new representation, append evidence, propose a relationship, record a correction, or preserve an uninterpreted source.

Not every captured item must become a durable domain object.

## Capture and Source

A **Source** is the origin from which captured information was obtained.

A Source may be:

- the Person;
- another Person or Party;
- an authorized User or agent;
- an external system;
- a device or sensor;
- an imported document or record;
- prior system state;
- or Parallel You's own inference.

Source identity forms part of the meaning and authority of captured information.

The system shall not represent information obtained from another source as though the Person explicitly provided or accepted it.

Where the Source is unknown, disputed, or only partially known, that uncertainty shall be preserved.

## Capture and Provenance

**Provenance** describes how information entered the system and how its representation has changed.

Relevant provenance may include:

- Source;
- capture method;
- capture Time;
- represented Time;
- original expression or record;
- transformations applied;
- interpretations introduced;
- confidence and uncertainty;
- authorizing User or agent;
- and later corrections or confirmations.

Provenance should be sufficient to distinguish evidence from interpretation where that distinction may affect action, trust, or revision.

Parallel You shall not discard provenance merely because a normalized representation is easier to process.

## Capture and Expression

An **Expression** is information communicated by the Person or another source in words, signals, selections, gestures, or another interpretable form.

An Expression may be:

- deliberate or incidental;
- complete or fragmentary;
- literal, figurative, tentative, or exploratory;
- ambiguous;
- emotionally charged;
- self-contradictory;
- or dependent upon prior Context.

Capture should preserve the Expression closely enough that later interpretation can be reconsidered when useful.

Parallel You shall not assume that every Expression is a command, declaration of fact, adopted Intention, accepted Commitment, or stable preference.

## Capture and Observation

An **Observation** is a representation of available evidence concerning a condition, Event, Activity, state, or Outcome.

Observation may be direct, reported, imported, instrumented, or inferred from other evidence.

Observation is distinct from interpretation.

Device activity, application state, location, calendar state, or sensor data may provide evidence without conclusively establishing human Activity, Attention, Energy, Intention, or experience.

Parallel You shall preserve the limits of what an Observation supports.

## Capture and Inference

An **Inference** is a conclusion or provisional interpretation derived from available information rather than explicitly supplied by an authoritative source.

Inference may help Parallel You:

- classify information;
- identify possible relationships;
- estimate missing properties;
- recognize patterns;
- or reduce the burden of manual entry.

An Inference shall remain distinguishable from declared, observed, or imported information.

The system shall represent confidence and material alternatives where uncertainty affects use.

Repeated behaviour may support an inference, but it shall not conclusively establish Intention, Commitment, preference, value, Motivation, or identity.

## Capture and Assumption

An **Assumption** is information provisionally introduced to permit interpretation, Planning, or action in the absence of sufficient evidence.

Assumptions may be useful, but they are not observations or facts.

Where an Assumption materially affects a Plan, Recommendation, or automated action, Parallel You should expose it to review.

An Assumption shall not silently become confirmed merely through repetition or continued use.

## Capture and Classification

Classification associates captured information with one or more concepts, categories, or operational roles.

Classification may be:

- explicit;
- suggested;
- inferred;
- provisional;
- partial;
- disputed;
- or deferred.

Captured information may fit several classifications at once or none presently available.

Parallel You should permit low-cost Capture before classification when classification would impose delay, burden, or invented precision.

The system shall not force an Intention into a Commitment, an Activity into a Task, an Event into an Activity, or a preference into a requirement merely to satisfy a schema.

## Capture and Normalization

**Normalization** transforms captured information into a form that supports comparison, retrieval, reasoning, or interoperability.

Normalization may concern:

- names and identifiers;
- dates, Times, and time zones;
- units and scales;
- references to Persons or organizations;
- recurring expressions;
- Activity descriptions;
- or external data formats.

Normalization may add operational value without increasing factual certainty.

The normalized form shall not silently replace relevant meaning from the original Source.

Where normalization requires interpretation, the introduced interpretation shall remain revisable.

## Capture and Resolution

**Resolution** associates captured information with an existing representation or determines that a new representation may be needed.

Resolution may concern:

- which Person, Activity, Plan, Intention, or Commitment is referenced;
- whether two expressions refer to the same thing;
- whether an Occurrence belongs to an Activity Definition;
- or which Context applies.

Resolution may be certain, probable, ambiguous, or deferred.

Parallel You shall not merge representations merely because their labels are similar.

Where an incorrect merge would materially distort meaning or action, ambiguity should remain visible until sufficiently resolved.

## Capture and Creation

Capture may result in the creation of a new representation.

Creation should occur when the captured information and its intended use justify a distinct representation.

The system should avoid creating unnecessary objects from conversational fragments, duplicated imports, transient states, or speculative interpretations.

A newly created representation may remain incomplete.

Capture shall not require every property needed for later Planning, Recommendation, or Tracking to be supplied at creation.

## Capture and Update

Capture may add evidence to or revise an existing representation.

An Update may:

- fill an unknown property;
- add a new observation;
- record a changed state;
- correct an error;
- revise an interpretation;
- add or remove a relationship;
- or supersede an earlier representation.

New information shall not silently overwrite relevant historical, contradictory, or differently sourced information.

Where information changes over Time, Parallel You should distinguish change in the represented world from correction of the record.

## Original and Derived Information

Parallel You should preserve a meaningful distinction between:

- original Source material;
- normalized information;
- resolved references;
- inferred properties;
- derived summaries;
- and operational representations.

Derived information may be more useful for system operation, but it does not replace the evidentiary role of its Source.

When a derived representation is corrected, the original Source should remain unchanged unless the Source itself is being corrected through an authorized process.

## Temporal Context

Capture may involve several distinct Times:

- when information was expressed or produced;
- when an Event or Activity occurred;
- when the system received the information;
- when the information was interpreted;
- and the Time to which the information presently applies.

These Times shall not be presumed identical.

Late Capture does not imply late occurrence.

The time at which a record was created shall not silently become the time at which its subject occurred.

Temporal precision shall not exceed the available evidence.

## Context

Captured information may depend upon conversational, personal, social, organizational, environmental, or temporal Context.

Context may affect:

- meaning;
- scope;
- authority;
- relevance;
- sensitivity;
- classification;
- and appropriate retention or use.

Parallel You should preserve Context where losing it would materially change interpretation.

The system shall not generalize information from one Context to another merely because the same Person is involved.

## Incompleteness

Captured information may be incomplete.

Incompleteness may concern:

- identity;
- meaning;
- scope;
- Time;
- conditions;
- relationships;
- confidence;
- or intended use.

Incomplete information may still possess substantial value.

Parallel You should preserve unknowns explicitly where they matter rather than preventing Capture or inventing values.

The system shall not require the Person to complete administrative detail before preserving a thought, need, or observation.

## Ambiguity

Captured information may support more than one reasonable interpretation.

Ambiguity may be resolved through Context, existing representations, clarification, later evidence, or explicit choice.

It may also remain unresolved.

Where ambiguity does not materially affect present use, Parallel You may defer clarification.

Where ambiguity materially affects authority, obligation, safety, identity, or consequential action, the system should not silently choose an interpretation.

## Contradiction

Captured information may conflict with existing information or with other information captured at the same time.

A contradiction may indicate:

- changed circumstances;
- different Contexts;
- different Sources or perspectives;
- correction;
- uncertainty;
- misunderstanding;
- or genuine disagreement.

Parallel You shall not automatically discard older or less confident information merely to produce a single consistent account.

Relevant contradiction should remain visible until it can be interpreted or no longer matters.

## Clarification

**Clarification** seeks additional information needed to interpret or use captured information appropriately.

Clarification may concern:

- meaning;
- identity;
- scope;
- Time;
- classification;
- authority;
- conditions;
- or desired system action.

Parallel You should request clarification only when its expected value justifies the interruption and burden.

Clarification may be immediate, deferred, optional, or unnecessary.

The system should not convert Capture into an interrogation merely because additional detail might someday be useful.

## Confirmation

**Confirmation** is an explicit or otherwise sufficiently authoritative indication that a proposed interpretation, relationship, or action is accepted as accurate or intended.

Confirmation may apply to only part of captured information.

Silence, continued conversation, or failure to correct the system shall not automatically constitute Confirmation.

Confirmation of an interpretation does not necessarily confirm every derived inference or authorize every possible use.

The level of Confirmation required should reflect the consequence and reversibility of the resulting action.

## Capture Burden

**Capture Burden** is the Time, Energy, Attention, interruption, and administrative effort required to provide or preserve information.

Capture Burden may arise from:

- excessive required fields;
- repeated classification;
- frequent prompts;
- correction of poor inference;
- duplication across systems;
- or demands for precision the Person cannot usefully provide.

Parallel You should minimize Capture Burden while preserving information needed for trustworthy operation.

The system shall not demand more Energy or Attention to record an Activity, state, or need than the resulting support is reasonably expected to return.

## Progressive Capture

**Progressive Capture** permits a representation to begin with minimal information and gain structure when additional detail becomes useful.

For example, a fragment may later become:

- an Intention;
- an Activity;
- a Commitment;
- an Event;
- part of a Plan;
- or a record retained only for Reflection.

Progressive Capture should preserve continuity between earlier and later representations without pretending that the earlier meaning was more precise than it was.

The system may suggest enrichment, but the Person shall not be required to complete it without sufficient reason.

## Passive and Active Capture

**Active Capture** occurs when the Person or another authorized source deliberately provides information to Parallel You.

**Passive Capture** occurs when information is collected without a distinct contemporaneous act of entry by the Person.

Passive Capture may reduce burden but introduces concerns involving:

- awareness;
- authority;
- privacy;
- interpretation;
- proportionality;
- and retention.

Parallel You shall not presume that authorization to access a source grants unrestricted authority to interpret, retain, combine, or act upon everything available from it.

## Imported Information

Imported information originates in another system, document, dataset, or service.

An import may preserve or transform:

- identifiers;
- provenance;
- timestamps;
- recurrence;
- relationships;
- confidence;
- and source-specific meaning.

Imported records may be incomplete, duplicated, outdated, inconsistent, or governed by semantics different from Parallel You's.

Availability through an integration shall not be treated as proof of accuracy, relevance, or the Person's endorsement.

## Consent and Authority

Capture shall occur within appropriate authority.

Authority may depend upon:

- the Person's delegation;
- the acting User;
- the Source;
- the type and sensitivity of information;
- the intended use;
- retention;
- and whether external action may follow.

Authority to Capture does not necessarily imply authority to infer, disclose, recommend, automate, or retain indefinitely.

Delegation shall remain bounded by purpose and scope.

## Privacy and Sensitivity

Captured information may concern private, sensitive, intimate, regulated, or consequential aspects of the Person or others.

Parallel You should collect and retain only the detail justified by its purpose and the Person's choices.

Sensitivity may affect:

- whether Capture is appropriate;
- required Confirmation;
- access;
- retention;
- display;
- inference;
- combination with other information;
- and external use.

The system shall not infer that information is harmless merely because it was easy to obtain.

## Retention

Captured information may be retained temporarily, durably, until a condition is met, or not at all beyond immediate processing.

Retention should reflect:

- the Person's expectations and authority;
- the information's purpose;
- sensitivity;
- expected future value;
- legal or institutional requirements;
- and the cost or risk of continued storage.

Durable retention shall not be the automatic consequence of every interaction.

Where appropriate, the Person should be able to inspect, correct, restrict, or remove retained representations.

## Side Effects

Capture may produce side effects such as:

- creating or updating representations;
- linking information;
- initiating clarification;
- affecting a Plan or Recommendation;
- creating a reminder;
- or triggering authorized automation.

The system shall distinguish preserving information from acting upon it.

Capture alone shall not authorize consequential, irreversible, external, or person-directed action.

Potential side effects should be proportionate, visible where relevant, and governed by applicable delegation.

## Error and Correction

Capture may fail through omission, duplication, distortion, misclassification, incorrect resolution, unsupported inference, or technical error.

Parallel You should make correction easier than recreating the affected information where practical.

A correction may revise the system's representation without claiming that the original Source said something different.

Corrections should preserve relevant provenance and downstream relationships.

Where an error affected a Plan, Recommendation, or action, the system should make that consequence discoverable and support appropriate revision.

## Failure and Recovery

Capture may be interrupted or incomplete because of:

- lost connectivity;
- unavailable sources;
- insufficient authority;
- parsing failure;
- ambiguity;
- conflicting information;
- or insufficient Person capacity.

The system should preserve recoverable partial work where doing so is safe and useful.

Failure to classify, normalize, or resolve information shall not necessarily mean failure to preserve it.

Parallel You shall communicate material Capture failure without implying that the Person failed to express themselves correctly.

## Lifecycle

A Capture may be:

- initiated;
- received;
- preserved;
- interpreted;
- classified;
- normalized;
- resolved;
- awaiting clarification;
- confirmed;
- applied;
- corrected;
- superseded;
- rejected;
- expired;
- or discarded.

Not every Capture passes through every state.

Different products of the same Capture may possess different states. Original information may be preserved while a proposed interpretation is rejected or revised.

Lifecycle state represents operational processing and shall not erase provenance or uncertainty.

## Relationships

Capture may:

- receive information from a Person, User, Party, agent, device, or external system;
- preserve Expressions and Observations;
- produce provisional classifications, Inferences, and Assumptions;
- create or update representations of domain concepts;
- request Clarification or Confirmation;
- provide inputs to Planning, Recommendation, Reflection, and Tracking;
- initiate bounded side effects under delegated authority;
- and produce provenance through which later representations may be understood and revised.

These relationships support continuity and assistance. They do not make captured information equivalent to the Person or to reality.

## Principles

- Capture should preserve useful meaning before demanding structure.
- The burden of Capture should remain proportional to the support it enables.
- Original, normalized, inferred, assumed, and confirmed information remain distinguishable.
- Provenance, Context, Time, and uncertainty form part of meaning.
- Incomplete or ambiguous information may still be worth preserving.
- Clarification should be driven by present consequence and value, not schema completion.
- Capture and action are distinct capabilities.
- Passive and imported Capture require bounded authority and careful interpretation.
- Correction should revise representations without rewriting Sources.
- Capture exists to reduce cognitive and administrative burden, not create it.

## Invariants

Every implementation of Parallel You shall preserve the following invariants:

- Captured information shall not be treated as equivalent to the Person, the represented world, or established fact.
- Information from another Source shall not be represented as explicitly provided or accepted by the Person.
- Original, observed, imported, normalized, inferred, assumed, and confirmed information shall remain distinguishable where the difference affects use.
- An Expression shall not automatically be treated as a command, fact, adopted Intention, accepted Commitment, stable preference, or authorization.
- Repeated behaviour shall not, by itself, conclusively establish Intention, Commitment, preference, value, Motivation, or identity.
- Device, application, calendar, sensor, or timer state shall not be treated as conclusive evidence of human Activity, Attention, Energy, or experience.
- Unknown information shall not be silently replaced with invented precision.
- Ambiguity shall not be silently resolved where the choice materially affects authority, obligation, safety, identity, or consequential action.
- Similar labels shall not, by themselves, establish that two representations refer to the same thing.
- Classification shall not force an Intention into a Commitment, an Activity into a Task, an Event into an Activity, or a preference into a requirement.
- Normalization shall not silently discard relevant meaning or increase apparent certainty.
- New information shall not silently overwrite relevant history, contradiction, provenance, or differently sourced information.
- Record creation Time shall not automatically be represented as occurrence Time.
- Silence, continued interaction, or failure to correct shall not automatically constitute Confirmation.
- Authorization to access a Source shall not imply unrestricted authority to retain, combine, infer from, disclose, or act upon its information.
- Capture alone shall not authorize consequential, irreversible, external, or person-directed action.
- Failure to classify, normalize, or resolve information shall not necessarily prevent its preservation.
- Capture shall not impose greater Time, Energy, Attention, or administrative burden than the expected value of the resulting support.
- The Person shall retain appropriate authority to inspect, correct, restrict, or remove representations produced through Capture.
