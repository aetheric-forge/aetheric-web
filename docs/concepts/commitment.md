# Commitment

## Definition

A **Commitment** is an Intention to which the Person has attached, accepted, or acknowledged an obligation.

A Commitment represents more than a desired outcome. It expresses that the Person believes something is owed, required, promised, expected, or deliberately undertaken.

The obligation may be owed:

- to the Person themselves;
- to another Person;
- to a group or institution;
- to a project;
- to a role;
- or under an agreement, responsibility, or governing rule.

## Purpose

The Commitment concept allows Parallel You to distinguish obligations from interests, possibilities, preferences, and aspirations.

Commitments may influence:

- planning;
- prioritization;
- scheduling;
- conflict detection;
- recommendation;
- communication;
- renegotiation;
- and reflection.

Parallel You shall help the Person understand and govern Commitments without treating every Intention as an obligation.

## Commitment and Intention

Every Commitment involves an Intention, but not every Intention is a Commitment.

An Intention may express something the Person wishes to bring about. A Commitment additionally expresses that the Person considers themselves responsible for acting, delivering, preserving, or responding.

The distinction may depend upon the Person’s understanding rather than an externally observable property.

Parallel You may ask whether an Intention is a Commitment when the distinction materially affects planning. It shall not silently promote an Intention into an obligation.

## Commitment and Desire

A Person may desire an outcome without committing to pursue it.

A Person may also accept a Commitment without desiring the required Activity or Outcome.

Desire and obligation may support one another, conflict, or remain unrelated.

Parallel You shall not infer willingness, enthusiasm, or personal importance merely from the existence of a Commitment.

## Commitment and Activity

A Commitment may be satisfied through one or more Activities.

The Commitment represents the obligation. Activities represent actions through which the Person may respond to it.

Changing an Activity or Plan does not necessarily alter the Commitment.

An Activity may contribute to several Commitments, and a Commitment may remain valid even when the appropriate Activity has not yet been identified.

The system shall not confuse the means chosen to address a Commitment with the Commitment itself.

## Commitment and Task

A Task may operationalize part or all of a Commitment.

Completing a Task does not necessarily satisfy the Commitment unless the applicable satisfaction conditions have been met.

Conversely, a Commitment may be satisfied without completing every Task originally associated with it.

Tasks are administrative representations of action. They do not define the obligation.

## Commitment and Plan

A Plan proposes how the Person may honour one or more Commitments under anticipated conditions.

A Plan is not itself proof that the Commitment can be satisfied.

When available capacity is insufficient, Parallel You shall expose the conflict rather than construct a Plan that quietly assumes impossible performance.

Revising a Plan may preserve the Commitment.

Revising, renegotiating, releasing, or ending a Commitment may invalidate related Plans.

## Source

A Commitment may arise from:

- an explicit promise;
- an agreement;
- acceptance of a request;
- responsibility for a role;
- participation in an institution;
- a legal or contractual obligation;
- responsibility for another Person or dependent;
- a deadline imposed by circumstances;
- a personal decision;
- or another recognized source of obligation.

The source of a Commitment forms part of its meaning.

Parallel You shall preserve whether a Commitment was:

- explicitly accepted;
- assigned;
- inferred;
- imported;
- suggested;
- disputed;
- or recorded with uncertain provenance.

An inferred Commitment shall not be represented as explicitly accepted.

## Parties

A Commitment may involve one or more Parties.

A Party may be:

- the Person;
- another Person;
- a group;
- an organization;
- an institution;
- or another identifiable authority or beneficiary.

Parties may hold different expectations concerning the Commitment.

Parallel You shall not assume that its representation of the Commitment is shared or accepted by every Party.

A record of another Party’s expectation is not proof that the Person accepted the obligation.

## Scope

A Commitment may define:

- an expected Outcome;
- required or prohibited Activities;
- a recipient or beneficiary;
- a deadline or relevant interval;
- conditions;
- quality expectations;
- recurrence;
- dependencies;
- consequences;
- and satisfaction criteria.

Scope may be explicit, implicit, disputed, or unknown.

Unclear scope is a property of the Commitment, not an invitation for the system to invent precision.

Parallel You may recommend clarification when uncertainty creates planning risk.

## Strength

Commitments may differ in the degree of obligation the Person recognizes.

A Commitment may be:

- tentative;
- conditional;
- ordinary;
- firm;
- or non-negotiable under present circumstances.

Commitment strength is distinct from urgency, importance, duration, and effort.

A strong Commitment may not be urgent.

An urgent Commitment may have been weakly or involuntarily accepted.

Parallel You shall not collapse these properties into a single priority value when doing so would obscure meaningful differences.

## Conditions

A Commitment may be conditional.

Conditions may determine:

- whether the Commitment becomes active;
- what the Person is required to do;
- when performance is expected;
- whether the Commitment may be satisfied;
- or whether the Commitment remains applicable.

A conditional Commitment shall not be treated as presently actionable unless its activating conditions are satisfied or reasonably anticipated.

The system shall preserve uncertainty when the state of a condition is unknown.

## Deadlines

A **Deadline** is a temporal constraint associated with a Commitment, Activity, Outcome, or external consequence.

A Commitment may exist without a Deadline.

A Deadline may be:

- externally imposed;
- negotiated;
- self-selected;
- estimated;
- provisional;
- or inferred.

Parallel You shall preserve the source and flexibility of a Deadline.

A self-selected planning target shall not be represented as an externally imposed obligation.

Passing a Deadline does not automatically end the Commitment. It may instead alter its status, feasibility, consequences, or required response.

## Satisfaction

A Commitment is **Satisfied** when its applicable obligation has been fulfilled according to the best available understanding of its scope and conditions.

Satisfaction may be:

- complete;
- partial;
- provisional;
- recurring;
- acknowledged by relevant Parties;
- or disputed.

Completion of associated Activities may provide evidence of satisfaction but shall not conclusively establish it.

Parallel You shall preserve uncertainty or disagreement concerning satisfaction when relevant.

## Renegotiation

A Commitment may be renegotiated when its scope, timing, conditions, or feasibility changes.

Renegotiation may result in:

- a revised Deadline;
- reduced or expanded scope;
- a different expected Outcome;
- reassignment;
- suspension;
- replacement;
- or release.

Renegotiation is a legitimate response to changed information or insufficient capacity.

Parallel You should surface the need for renegotiation early enough for the Person to act. It shall not conceal infeasibility by repeatedly rescheduling associated Activities.

## Release

A Person may be released from a Commitment by:

- agreement among relevant Parties;
- fulfilment of a terminating condition;
- withdrawal by an appropriate authority;
- transfer of responsibility;
- expiration;
- or another recognized change in obligation.

Release is distinct from Satisfaction.

A released Commitment no longer requires performance even though its original Outcome may not have been achieved.

## Withdrawal and Abandonment

A Person may withdraw from or abandon a Commitment.

Withdrawal describes the Person’s decision not to continue accepting or performing the obligation.

Withdrawal may have interpersonal, institutional, practical, or legal consequences. Parallel You may help the Person understand and manage those consequences, but it shall not convert them into moral judgment.

A Commitment does not cease to have existed merely because the Person no longer accepts it.

## Breach

A **Breach** occurs when a Commitment remains applicable and the required obligation is not satisfied under its governing conditions.

A missed Deadline may provide evidence of Breach but does not establish one in every case.

Breach may be:

- remediable;
- accepted or waived;
- disputed;
- partial;
- continuing;
- or superseded by renegotiation.

Parallel You may identify a possible Breach and its consequences. It shall distinguish operational assessment from moral judgment.

The system shall not label the Person as unreliable, irresponsible, or deficient merely because a Commitment was breached.

## Feasibility

A Commitment may exceed the Person’s available or anticipated capacity.

Parallel You shall not assume that all accepted Commitments can be honoured simultaneously.

When Commitments compete for insufficient time, Energy, Attention, or resources, the system should:

- identify the conflict;
- preserve the affected Commitments;
- estimate relevant consequences;
- and support prioritization, renegotiation, delegation, deferral, or withdrawal.

An infeasible collection of Commitments is a planning condition, not evidence that the Person should simply exert more effort.

## Delegation

Responsibility for Activities associated with a Commitment may be delegated.

Delegating an Activity does not necessarily transfer or end the underlying Commitment.

The Person may remain responsible for:

- selecting the delegate;
- communicating expectations;
- monitoring progress;
- accepting the Outcome;
- or responding if delegation fails.

Parallel You shall preserve whether delegation transfers the obligation, transfers only performance, or merely requests assistance.

## Recurrence

A Commitment may recur.

A recurring Commitment may create distinct obligations for each applicable interval or condition.

Examples include:

- attending a weekly meeting;
- paying a monthly invoice;
- providing ongoing care;
- or maintaining a service.

Failure to satisfy one Occurrence shall not silently transform every future Occurrence into overdue debt.

Parallel You shall distinguish the continuing Commitment from its individual expected performances.

## Lifecycle

A Commitment may be:

- proposed;
- recognized;
- accepted;
- active;
- conditional;
- suspended;
- under renegotiation;
- satisfied;
- released;
- withdrawn;
- breached;
- disputed;
- superseded;
- or no longer applicable.

Not every Commitment passes through every state.

More than one description may apply at the same time. For example, a Commitment may be active, disputed, and under renegotiation.

Lifecycle state represents the system’s best operational understanding. It shall preserve uncertainty and disagreement where they exist.

## Relationships

A Commitment may:

- belong to a Person;
- arise from an Intention;
- involve one or more Parties;
- exist within a Context or role;
- possess Conditions or Deadlines;
- be served by Activities;
- appear within Plans;
- depend upon Events or other Commitments;
- require time, Energy, Attention, or resources;
- and produce Outcomes, consequences, or further Commitments.

These relationships support planning and governance. They do not determine the moral worth of the Person.

## Principles

- Commitments represent recognized obligations.
- Not every Intention is a Commitment.
- The source, Parties, scope, and conditions of a Commitment remain relevant.
- Obligation is distinct from desire, importance, urgency, and enthusiasm.
- Plans and Tasks serve Commitments but do not define them.
- Commitments may conflict with one another and exceed available capacity.
- Renegotiation, delegation, release, and withdrawal are legitimate responses to changing circumstances.
- Satisfaction, release, withdrawal, and Breach are distinct outcomes.
- Operational consequences may be represented without converting them into moral judgment.
- A Person’s worth is not determined by the number of Commitments they accept or satisfy.

## Invariants

Every implementation of Parallel You shall preserve the following invariants:

- A Commitment shall not be treated as equivalent to an Intention, Desire, Task, Activity, Plan, or Deadline.
- An inferred, assigned, or imported Commitment shall not be represented as explicitly accepted by the Person.
- Another Party’s expectation shall not, by itself, establish the Person’s acceptance.
- Unclear scope shall not be silently replaced with invented precision.
- Commitment strength, urgency, importance, effort, and duration shall remain distinguishable.
- A planning target shall not be represented as an externally imposed Deadline.
- Completion of an associated Activity or Task shall not automatically establish Satisfaction.
- Passing a Deadline shall not automatically end the Commitment.
- Insufficient capacity shall not be concealed through impossible planning.
- Repeated rescheduling shall not substitute indefinitely for identifying infeasibility or the need for renegotiation.
- Delegation of performance shall not be presumed to transfer the underlying obligation.
- Satisfaction, release, withdrawal, abandonment, and Breach shall remain distinguishable.
- Breach shall not be converted into a judgment concerning the Person’s character or worth.
- The Person shall retain authority to question, renegotiate, or withdraw from a Commitment, subject to consequences that remain distinct from the system’s judgment.
