# Plan

## Definition

A **Plan** is a provisional representation of how one or more Intentions or Commitments may be served under anticipated conditions.

A Plan relates possible Activities, Time, Energy, Attention, resources, constraints, and dependencies in order to make purposeful action easier to understand and undertake.

A Plan describes a presently considered course of action. It is neither a prediction nor a command.

The Person may accept, revise, partially follow, defer, replace, or reject a Plan.

## Purpose

The Plan concept allows Parallel You to:

- connect Intentions and Commitments with possible action;
- represent a coherent course through multiple Activities;
- test action against anticipated capacity and constraints;
- expose conflicts, dependencies, uncertainty, and alternatives;
- preserve continuity across interruption and changing circumstances;
- support coordination over Time;
- and provide a revisable basis for action and reflection.

The purpose of a Plan is not to prescribe the Person's life in advance.

Its purpose is to reduce the burden of deciding, remembering, arranging, and adapting while preserving the Person's authority.

## Plan and Intention

An Intention expresses direction.

A Plan proposes how that direction may be served.

An Intention may exist without a Plan. One Intention may be served by several alternative Plans, and one Plan may serve several Intentions.

Changing a Plan does not necessarily change the Intention it serves.

Following a Plan does not establish that the underlying Intention remains unchanged, and abandoning a Plan does not, by itself, abandon the Intention.

Parallel You shall not confuse the proposed means with the reason those means matter.

## Plan and Commitment

A Plan may propose how the Person can honour one or more Commitments.

The Commitment represents an obligation. The Plan represents a possible response to that obligation.

Creating or accepting a Plan does not prove that a Commitment is feasible.

When anticipated capacity is insufficient, the Plan should expose the conflict and may include renegotiation, delegation, clarification, or release among the possible responses.

Parallel You shall not use repeated replanning to conceal a Commitment that cannot reasonably be satisfied.

## Plan and Activity

A Plan may contain, relate, order, group, reserve capacity for, or choose among Activities.

An Activity describes something the Person may do. Its inclusion in a Plan describes a present proposal concerning that Activity.

An Activity may appear in more than one Plan. A Plan may also reserve capacity or establish conditions without identifying every Activity in advance.

Removing an Activity from a Plan does not necessarily make the Activity irrelevant, and completing an Activity does not necessarily complete the Plan.

Parallel You shall preserve the distinction between an Activity and its role within a particular Plan.

## Plan and Schedule

A Schedule is a temporal arrangement of Activities, Events, Commitments, or reserved Intervals.

A Schedule may form part of a Plan, but a Plan may also represent:

- choices and alternatives;
- dependencies;
- required conditions;
- capacity assumptions;
- stopping or review conditions;
- contingencies;
- and criteria for revision.

A Plan therefore need not assign every Activity to an exact Time.

A scheduled item expresses a temporal expectation. It does not prove that the Plan was accepted or that the Activity occurred.

## Plan and Task

A Task may operationalize part of a Plan.

A Plan is not a collection of Tasks merely because Tasks appear within it.

Plans may include open-ended Activities, rest, waiting, observation, transition, deliberation, protected capacity, and responses to conditions that cannot meaningfully be marked complete.

Completing every recorded Task does not necessarily satisfy the Plan's Intentions or Commitments.

Conversely, the Plan may cease to be useful before every associated Task is completed.

## Plan and Recommendation

A Recommendation may propose a Plan, propose a change to a Plan, or select an Activity from a Plan for the Person's consideration.

A recommended Plan is not an accepted Plan.

The system shall preserve whether a Plan was:

- created by the Person;
- jointly developed;
- generated by Parallel You;
- imported from another source;
- inferred from available evidence;
- or reconstructed from observed action.

Generating or presenting a Plan does not grant authority to carry it out.

## Plan and Prediction

A Plan describes what may be done.

A Prediction describes what the system expects may occur.

The two may inform one another but remain distinct.

A Person may adopt a Plan that the system considers unlikely. The system may predict an Activity without that Activity belonging to any Plan.

Parallel You shall not present planned outcomes, Activities, Durations, or capacity states as predictions unless predictive reasoning has separately supported them.

Likewise, a prediction shall not silently become a Plan.

## Plan and Record

A Plan is primarily prospective, but it may persist as a historical record after its relevant Time has passed.

A historical Plan records what was represented, proposed, or accepted at a particular time. It does not establish what occurred.

Observed Activities, Events, Outcomes, and conditions should remain distinguishable from the Plan against which they may later be compared.

Revising a current Plan shall not silently rewrite its earlier states where those states provide relevant context.

## Scope

A Plan may concern:

- a single Activity;
- part of a day;
- a journey or transition;
- a Project;
- an Intention or Commitment;
- a recurring responsibility;
- a Context or role;
- a response to a possible Event;
- or a longer period of the Person's life.

Scope may be narrow, broad, nested, overlapping, or conditional.

A broad Plan may contain or relate to more specific Plans. A specific Plan may serve several broader Plans without being exclusively owned by any one of them.

Parallel You shall not require every Plan to fit a single hierarchy.

## Horizon

A **Plan Horizon** is the span of future Time for which the Plan presently attempts to provide useful guidance.

The Horizon may be:

- explicitly bounded;
- defined by a Deadline or Event;
- rolling;
- conditional;
- approximate;
- or unknown.

The useful degree of detail commonly decreases as the Horizon extends.

Distant portions of a Plan should not be represented with the same presumed precision or stability as immediate portions when the evidence does not support it.

Parallel You may progressively clarify a Plan as relevant Time approaches.

## Elements

A Plan may include:

- related Intentions and Commitments;
- proposed Activities;
- expected Outcomes;
- temporal Allocations or constraints;
- anticipated Energy and Attention demands;
- required resources and conditions;
- ordering and dependencies;
- alternatives;
- contingencies;
- assumptions;
- uncertainties;
- review points;
- and completion, stopping, or supersession conditions.

No element is universally required.

The detail represented shall remain proportional to its expected planning value.

## Assumptions

A Plan depends upon assumptions about future conditions.

Assumptions may concern:

- available Time;
- Energy and Attention;
- Activity duration and demand;
- resources;
- other Persons;
- external Events;
- dependencies;
- and the continued relevance of Intentions or Commitments.

An assumption may be explicit, inferred, conventional, or unknown.

Where an assumption materially affects feasibility or risk, Parallel You should make it visible.

An unverified assumption shall not be silently converted into a fact.

## Constraints

A Plan may be shaped by constraints concerning:

- Time;
- capacity;
- sequence;
- location;
- resources;
- authority;
- obligation;
- safety;
- privacy;
- coordination;
- or external conditions.

Constraints may be hard, flexible, conditional, disputed, inferred, or unknown.

The system shall preserve the source and flexibility of a constraint where those properties affect available choices.

A preference shall not be represented as a requirement merely because the Plan presently accommodates it.

## Dependencies

A Plan may represent that one Activity, Event, condition, resource, or Outcome depends upon another.

A dependency may concern:

- possibility;
- sequence;
- information;
- authorization;
- resources;
- capacity;
- or usefulness.

Temporal ordering alone does not establish dependency.

Unknown or uncertain dependencies should remain distinguishable from confirmed dependencies.

Parallel You should avoid constructing false certainty by forcing every relationship into a dependency graph.

## Alternatives

A Plan may preserve more than one possible course of action.

Alternatives may be:

- mutually exclusive;
- conditionally selected;
- partially compatible;
- ranked;
- equivalent for present purposes;
- or left deliberately unresolved.

The existence of alternatives does not make a Plan incomplete.

Parallel You may help compare alternatives, but it shall not silently discard meaningful options merely to produce a single apparently decisive course.

## Contingency

A **Contingency** describes a possible response to a condition, Event, failure, delay, or change relevant to the Plan.

A contingency may specify:

- an alternative Activity;
- a reduced scope;
- a revised sequence;
- use of reserved capacity;
- a communication or renegotiation;
- a review;
- or deliberate cessation.

Not every uncertainty requires a contingency.

The administrative and cognitive cost of preparing for a possibility should remain proportional to its consequence, likelihood, and relevance.

## Feasibility

**Plan Feasibility** is the degree to which available evidence supports that the Plan may be carried out under its anticipated conditions.

Feasibility may depend upon:

- available Time;
- Energy and Attention;
- resources;
- Activity demands;
- dependencies;
- external cooperation;
- constraints;
- uncertainty;
- and reserved capacity.

Feasibility is contextual, provisional, and may be partial.

A Plan may be logically coherent while practically infeasible.

A feasible Plan is not necessarily desirable, acceptable, sustainable, or chosen.

Parallel You shall not obtain apparent feasibility by ignoring recovery, transition, uncertainty, conflicting Commitments, or ordinary life.

## Capacity and Reserve

A Plan may allocate or preserve portions of the Person's anticipated capacity.

Capacity may include Time, Energy, Attention, resources, and context-dependent ability.

A resilient Plan should distinguish capacity expected to be usable from capacity intentionally preserved for:

- recovery;
- uncertainty;
- essential responsibilities;
- interruption;
- spontaneous choice;
- and changing conditions.

Parallel You shall not treat all estimated capacity as available for allocation.

A Plan that occupies every apparent Interval or consumes every estimated reserve shall not be presumed optimal.

## Flexibility

**Plan Flexibility** describes how and to what degree the Plan may change while remaining useful to its Intentions or Commitments.

Flexibility may apply to:

- Activity selection;
- sequence;
- timing;
- duration;
- scope;
- quality;
- location;
- delegation;
- or expected Outcome.

Different parts of a Plan may possess different flexibility.

Flexibility is not vagueness, and precision is not rigidity.

Parallel You should preserve known flexibility rather than hardening a Plan merely because the software can represent exact values.

## Detail and Granularity

A Plan may exist at different levels of detail.

Greater detail may improve execution, estimation, coordination, or confidence. It may also increase planning cost, maintenance burden, and brittleness.

Parallel You should introduce detail when it helps the Person decide or act.

The system shall not require distant, uncertain, familiar, exploratory, or low-consequence action to be decomposed beyond its practical value.

A Plan may deliberately leave later decisions unresolved until better information becomes available.

## Acceptance

A Plan is **Accepted** when the Person or an appropriately authorized Party adopts it as a present basis for action.

Acceptance may be:

- explicit;
- partial;
- conditional;
- provisional;
- delegated within a defined scope;
- or unknown.

Viewing, discussing, editing, or failing to reject a Plan does not necessarily constitute acceptance.

Acceptance of a Plan does not make each Activity mandatory or transfer unlimited authority to Parallel You.

The Person may withdraw acceptance without first proving that the Plan is defective.

## Execution

**Plan Execution** is the evolving relationship between an accepted Plan and action taken under changing conditions.

Execution may include:

- undertaking planned Activities;
- selecting among alternatives;
- responding to Events;
- revising assumptions;
- changing sequence or scope;
- pausing;
- seeking clarification;
- or abandoning the Plan.

Execution is not mechanical compliance.

Observed action may differ from the Plan for reasons the system does not know.

Parallel You shall support the Person during execution without treating deviation as disobedience or failure.

## Progress

**Plan Progress** describes available evidence concerning how the Plan's relevant conditions, Activities, or Outcomes have changed.

Progress may be:

- qualitative;
- partial;
- nonlinear;
- uncertain;
- reversed;
- blocked;
- or not meaningfully measurable.

Activity completion may provide evidence of Progress but shall not be its universal measure.

A Plan serving maintenance, recovery, learning, care, or exploration may progress without accumulating completed Tasks.

Parallel You shall not infer the value of a Plan solely from the volume or speed of recorded Activity.

## Deviation

A **Deviation** is a relevant difference between the Plan and observed or chosen action or conditions.

A Deviation may arise from:

- new information;
- changed capacity;
- interruption;
- an inaccurate estimate;
- a deliberate choice;
- an unexpected opportunity;
- an external Event;
- or a change in Intention or Commitment.

A Deviation is evidence for interpretation, not proof of error.

The system should distinguish harmless variation, useful adaptation, material conflict, and unknown difference where possible.

Deviation shall not automatically generate corrective action.

## Revision

A Plan may be revised when understanding, conditions, capacity, Intentions, Commitments, or available choices change.

Revision may alter:

- scope;
- Activities;
- sequence;
- timing;
- assumptions;
- constraints;
- alternatives;
- contingencies;
- or expected Outcomes.

Revision is part of planning, not evidence that planning failed.

Where continuity matters, Parallel You should preserve what changed, why it changed when known, and which earlier state the revision replaced.

A materially different Plan may supersede an earlier Plan rather than silently becoming the same Plan.

## Review

A Plan may define conditions or Times at which its continued usefulness should be reconsidered.

Review may be appropriate when:

- material assumptions change;
- a dependency is resolved or fails;
- capacity differs from expectation;
- a relevant Event occurs;
- progress becomes uncertain;
- the Plan approaches its Horizon;
- or the Person requests reconsideration.

Review does not require optimization.

The outcome of Review may be continuation, revision, replacement, suspension, or cessation.

Parallel You should avoid demanding review more frequently than its expected value justifies.

## Completion and Cessation

A Plan may be considered complete when its applicable completion conditions have been satisfied.

A Plan may also cease to guide action because it is:

- no longer useful;
- rejected;
- abandoned;
- superseded;
- expired;
- invalidated by changed conditions;
- or rendered irrelevant by a changed Intention or Commitment.

Completion is distinct from cessation.

A Plan may cease without failure, and it may complete without every proposed Activity occurring.

The completion of a Plan does not conclusively establish satisfaction of every related Intention or Commitment.

## Lifecycle

A Plan may be:

- captured;
- proposed;
- generated;
- under consideration;
- accepted;
- active;
- paused;
- blocked;
- under review;
- revised;
- completed;
- rejected;
- abandoned;
- superseded;
- expired;
- or no longer relevant.

Not every Plan passes through every state.

More than one description may apply at the same time. A Plan may, for example, be active, partially blocked, and under review.

Lifecycle state represents the system's operational understanding. It shall preserve uncertainty and disagreement where they exist.

## Relationships

A Plan may:

- belong to or be accepted by a Person;
- serve one or more Intentions;
- address one or more Commitments;
- contain or relate Activities;
- include a Schedule or temporal Allocations;
- anticipate Energy and Attention requirements;
- depend upon resources, conditions, Events, or Outcomes;
- preserve alternatives and contingencies;
- be proposed through a Recommendation;
- guide delegated action within an authorized scope;
- produce revisions and historical states;
- and be compared with Observations without being confused with them.

These relationships support action, adaptation, and reflection. They do not grant the Plan authority over the Person.

## Principles

- A Plan is a provisional course of action, not a command or prediction.
- Plans serve Intentions and Commitments; they do not replace them.
- A Schedule or Task list may form part of a Plan but is not the whole Plan.
- Plans may preserve alternatives, uncertainty, flexibility, and unresolved decisions.
- Feasibility is contextual, provisional, and distinct from desirability or acceptance.
- Acceptance, execution, observation, and completion remain distinguishable.
- Revision and deviation are ordinary features of planning under changing conditions.
- Resilient Plans preserve capacity for recovery, uncertainty, and choice.
- Planning detail should reduce the burden of action rather than create administrative debt.
- The Person retains authority to accept, revise, disregard, or abandon a Plan.

## Invariants

Every implementation of Parallel You shall preserve the following invariants:

- A Plan shall not be treated as equivalent to an Intention, Commitment, Activity, Task, Schedule, Recommendation, Prediction, or record of what occurred.
- A proposed or generated Plan shall not be represented as accepted by the Person.
- Acceptance of a Plan shall not be inferred solely from viewing, discussing, editing, or failing to reject it.
- Inclusion of an Activity in a Plan shall not be represented as evidence that the Activity occurred.
- A Plan shall not be treated as feasible merely because its Activities fit within apparently open Calendar Time.
- Feasibility shall not be manufactured by ignoring Energy, Attention, transition, recovery, uncertainty, conflicting Commitments, or required resources.
- Planned capacity shall not automatically include every unallocated Interval or every estimated reserve.
- Assumptions, estimates, and inferred constraints shall not be represented as established facts.
- Preferences shall not be silently hardened into requirements.
- Temporal order shall not, by itself, be represented as dependency.
- Meaningful alternatives shall not be silently discarded merely to produce a single course of action.
- Deviation from a Plan shall not automatically be represented as error, failure, or a need for correction.
- Activity completion shall not be treated as the sole measure of Progress.
- Completing a Plan shall not automatically establish satisfaction of every related Intention or Commitment.
- Revision shall not silently rewrite relevant prior Plan states.
- Replanning shall not be used indefinitely to conceal infeasibility or avoid necessary renegotiation.
- Planning detail and review shall not impose greater burden than the value they provide.
- The Plan and the system's optimization goals shall remain subordinate to the Person's agency.
