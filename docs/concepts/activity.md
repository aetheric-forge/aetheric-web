# Activity

## Definition

An **Activity** is a representation of something a Person may do, is doing, or has done.

An Activity gives operational form to action. It allows Parallel You to reason about what may be undertaken, what conditions it may require, what Intentions it may serve, and what occurred when the Person acted.

Activities may be brief or extended, deliberate or incidental, solitary or collaborative, productive or restorative.

Examples include:

- write an article;
- answer email;
- walk to the store;
- attend a meeting;
- study a chapter;
- rest;
- prepare a meal;
- repair a server;
- talk with a friend;
- and explore an unfamiliar idea.

## Purpose

The Activity concept allows Parallel You to:

- represent possible action;
- connect action to Intention;
- estimate required time, Energy, and Attention;
- recommend suitable action under present conditions;
- include action within a Plan;
- observe what the Person actually does;
- and compare anticipated requirements with experienced outcomes.

An Activity is not valuable merely because it produces a measurable output.

Rest, maintenance, exploration, transition, recreation, and care are legitimate Activities.

## Activity and Intention

An Intention expresses direction.

An Activity describes action that may serve that direction.

One Intention may be served by many Activities. One Activity may serve several Intentions. An Activity may also be undertaken without a previously recorded Intention.

Performing an Activity does not conclusively establish the Person’s underlying Intention.

Parallel You shall preserve the distinction between why an action matters and what action is performed.

## Activity and Task

A **Task** is an Activity represented as something to be completed.

Tasks typically possess a completion condition, expected outcome, or defined stopping point.

Not every Activity is a Task.

Activities such as resting, thinking, reading, practising, conversing, maintaining, or exploring may be worthwhile without reaching a terminal state.

Parallel You shall not force every Activity into a completed or incomplete classification.

## Activity and Event

An **Event** is something that occurs at a time.

An Event may involve the Person without representing action chosen or performed by them. Weather, a deadline, an interruption, a system failure, or another Person’s arrival may all be Events.

An Activity may produce Events, respond to Events, or occur during Events.

The occurrence of an Event shall not be confused with an Activity undertaken by the Person.

## Activity Definition and Occurrence

An **Activity Definition** describes a kind of action that may be undertaken.

An **Activity Occurrence** represents a particular performance or attempted performance of that action.

For example:

- “write documentation” may be an Activity Definition;
- “write documentation from 10:15 to 11:05 on Thursday” may be an Activity Occurrence.

An Activity Definition may exist without any Occurrence.

An Occurrence may be recorded without a prior Definition.

Multiple Occurrences may refer to the same Definition while differing in duration, conditions, outcomes, and experienced demand.

Parallel You shall preserve the distinction between the model of an Activity and evidence of a particular performance.

## Planned, Recommended, and Observed Activity

An Activity may appear in several operational contexts.

A **Planned Activity** is an Activity included within a Plan.

A **Recommended Activity** is an Activity proposed by Parallel You for consideration.

An **Observed Activity** is an Activity that available evidence indicates occurred.

These descriptions are not interchangeable.

Planning an Activity does not mean it occurred.

Recommending an Activity does not mean the Person accepted it.

Observing an Activity does not establish that it was planned or intended.

Parallel You shall preserve these distinctions when comparing intention, recommendation, decision, and action.

## Description

An Activity may be captured with incomplete information.

Its description may initially consist of only a phrase, note, or inferred category. Additional detail may be introduced when it becomes useful.

An Activity may include:

- a name or description;
- one or more related Intentions;
- an expected outcome;
- an estimated duration;
- required or preferred conditions;
- expected Energy demand;
- expected Attention demand;
- required resources;
- location or Context;
- dependencies;
- and a completion or stopping condition.

None of these properties is universally required.

The cost of describing an Activity shall remain proportional to the planning value of the information collected.

## Requirements

An Activity may require or prefer particular conditions.

Requirements may concern:

- time;
- duration;
- Energy;
- Attention;
- location;
- tools;
- information;
- another Person;
- privacy;
- connectivity;
- physical capability;
- emotional readiness;
- or completion of another Activity.

A requirement expresses a condition necessary for undertaking the Activity.

A preference expresses a condition that improves suitability without making the Activity impossible.

Parallel You shall distinguish requirements from preferences where that distinction affects recommendations or planning.

Unknown requirements shall remain unknown rather than being assumed absent.

## Demand

An Activity may place demands upon the Person.

Demand may include:

- physical exertion;
- cognitive effort;
- emotional labour;
- sustained Attention;
- social interaction;
- decision-making;
- transition cost;
- and recovery cost.

Demand is contextual.

The same Activity may impose different demands depending upon the Person’s condition, environment, familiarity, available support, or the particular Occurrence.

An estimated demand is a planning hypothesis, not an intrinsic and immutable property of the Activity.

## Duration

An Activity may have:

- a fixed duration;
- an estimated duration;
- a minimum useful duration;
- a maximum tolerable duration;
- an open-ended duration;
- or no meaningful duration estimate.

Duration may differ among Occurrences.

An Activity taking longer than estimated does not imply that the Activity or Person failed. It provides evidence with which the estimate and surrounding Plan may be revised.

Parallel You shall preserve uncertainty where duration cannot be known reliably.

## Granularity

Activities may be represented at different levels of granularity.

For example:

- publish a video;
- edit the video;
- correct the audio;
- remove background noise from the introduction.

A broad Activity may contain or relate to more specific Activities.

Decomposition should occur when it improves understanding, recommendation, execution, or estimation. It shall not be required merely to satisfy an arbitrary structural preference.

An Activity may remain broad until the Person needs a more specific next action.

## Outcome

An **Outcome** is a condition or result produced, preserved, or discovered through an Activity.

An Activity may:

- achieve its expected Outcome;
- partially achieve it;
- produce an unexpected Outcome;
- reveal that the Outcome is unnecessary;
- or produce no identifiable Outcome.

The value of an Activity shall not depend entirely upon achieving a predetermined Outcome.

Exploration, learning, care, recovery, and relationship-building may produce value that was not specified in advance or is not readily measurable.

## Completion

An Activity may be considered complete when an applicable completion condition has been satisfied.

Completion conditions may be:

- objective;
- subjective;
- externally determined;
- provisional;
- or absent.

Completion is distinct from stopping.

The Person may stop an Activity because it is complete, interrupted, no longer useful, no longer possible, or deliberately abandoned.

Parallel You shall not represent every stopped Activity as completed or every incomplete Activity as failed.

## Interruption and Continuation

An Activity Occurrence may be interrupted.

An interruption may be:

- expected or unexpected;
- voluntary or imposed;
- brief or indefinite;
- and relevant or irrelevant to future planning.

An interrupted Activity may later continue through the same or another Occurrence.

Continuation may incur transition and recovery costs. Parallel You should preserve enough continuity to help the Person resume without reconstructing the Activity from memory.

The system shall not assume that all interrupted Activities should be resumed.

## Concurrency

A Person may appear to participate in more than one Activity during the same interval.

Activities may:

- genuinely occur together;
- alternate rapidly;
- overlap because one requires intermittent involvement;
- or be recorded imprecisely.

Parallel You shall not presume that overlapping records represent equal or simultaneous Attention.

Concurrent Activity does not create additional human capacity.

## Recurrence

An Activity may recur.

Recurrence may be:

- scheduled;
- conditional;
- habitual;
- situational;
- or irregular.

A recurring Activity Definition may produce multiple distinct Occurrences.

Recurrence shall not imply that every Occurrence has identical demand, duration, importance, or Outcome.

Failure to perform one expected Occurrence shall not silently invalidate the recurring Activity or generate an accumulating moral debt.

## Lifecycle

An Activity may be:

- captured;
- clarified;
- available;
- planned;
- recommended;
- accepted;
- started;
- active;
- paused;
- interrupted;
- resumed;
- stopped;
- completed;
- abandoned;
- superseded;
- or no longer relevant.

These descriptions may apply differently to an Activity Definition and an Activity Occurrence.

Not every Activity passes through every state.

Lifecycle state represents the system’s operational understanding. It shall not be treated as a complete account of the Person’s experience.

## Relationships

An Activity may:

- serve one or more Intentions;
- satisfy or contribute to a Commitment;
- occur within a Context;
- require time, Energy, Attention, resources, or conditions;
- depend upon other Activities or Events;
- appear within a Plan;
- be proposed by a Recommendation;
- produce one or more Occurrences;
- and result in Outcomes and Observations.

These relationships support planning and reflection. They do not determine the Activity’s value.

## Principles

- Activities give operational form to action.
- An Activity is distinct from the Intention it may serve.
- Not every Activity is a Task.
- An Activity Definition is distinct from an Activity Occurrence.
- Planned, recommended, and observed Activities remain distinguishable.
- Demand and duration are contextual and uncertain.
- Activities may be useful without producing measurable output.
- Completion, stopping, interruption, and abandonment are distinct outcomes.
- Activity decomposition should reduce execution burden rather than increase administrative burden.
- Parallel You should preserve continuity across interruption without presuming that resumption is required.

## Invariants

Every implementation of Parallel You shall preserve the following invariants:

- An Activity shall not be treated as equivalent to an Intention, Task, Event, Outcome, or Activity Occurrence.
- Planning an Activity shall not be represented as evidence that it occurred.
- Recommending an Activity shall not be represented as acceptance by the Person.
- Observing an Activity shall not conclusively establish the Person’s Intention.
- Estimated duration and demand shall not be represented as objective or immutable facts.
- Unknown requirements shall not be silently treated as absent.
- Overlapping Activity Occurrences shall not be presumed to receive equal or simultaneous Attention.
- Stopping an Activity shall not necessarily mean completing it.
- An incomplete Activity shall not, by itself, be represented as failed.
- Interruption shall remain distinguishable from abandonment.
- Recurrence shall not transform missed Occurrences into accumulating moral debt.
- Restorative, exploratory, relational, and maintenance Activities shall remain valid without requiring conventional productive output.
- The administrative cost of representing an Activity shall remain proportional to the value that representation provides.
