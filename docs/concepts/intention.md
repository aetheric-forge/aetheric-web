# Intention

## Definition

An **Intention** is a representation of something a Person presently means, desires, expects, or is disposed to bring about.

An Intention gives direction to planning without necessarily specifying the activity, time, method, or commitment through which it may be realized.

Intentions may be precise or ambiguous, immediate or distant, temporary or enduring, independent or conditional.

Examples include:

- write an article;
- improve physical health;
- call a friend;
- maintain the garden;
- learn a language;
- rest this afternoon;
- investigate an unfamiliar subject;
- and avoid accepting additional work this week.

## Purpose

The Intention concept allows Parallel You to represent what matters before requiring the Person to determine exactly how or when it will happen.

An Intention may guide:

- capture;
- clarification;
- planning;
- prioritization;
- activity selection;
- recommendation;
- reflection;
- and revision.

Parallel You shall not require every Intention to become a scheduled activity or formal commitment.

## Intention and Desire

A **Desire** is something the Person wants or would prefer.

An Intention involves some degree of orientation toward bringing about or preserving an outcome. A Person may desire something without intending to act upon it.

The boundary between Desire and Intention may be uncertain.

Parallel You may invite clarification, but it shall not silently convert every expressed desire into an actionable obligation.

## Intention and Goal

A **Goal** describes an outcome against which progress or completion may be evaluated.

An Intention may contain, produce, or relate to a Goal, but the concepts are distinct.

An Intention may lack a measurable outcome.

For example, “spend more time reading” may express an Intention without defining a completion condition. A Goal such as “read twelve books this year” introduces a particular measure that may serve that Intention.

The Goal shall not be mistaken for the underlying reason it exists.

## Intention and Activity

An **Activity** is something the Person may do.

An Intention explains why an Activity may be considered. One Intention may be served by several Activities, and one Activity may serve several Intentions.

The Person may intend an outcome without yet knowing which Activity would advance it.

The system shall not require premature selection of an Activity merely to preserve an Intention.

## Intention and Commitment

A **Commitment** is an Intention to which the Person has attached, accepted, or acknowledged an obligation.

The obligation may arise from:

- a promise;
- an agreement;
- a responsibility;
- a deadline;
- a dependency;
- a role;
- or a deliberate decision by the Person.

Not every Intention is a Commitment.

Parallel You shall preserve this distinction so that interests, possibilities, aspirations, and curiosities do not acquire the moral or operational weight of obligations.

## Intention and Plan

A **Plan** proposes how one or more Intentions may be served under anticipated conditions.

An Intention may exist without a Plan.

A Plan may serve several Intentions.

Changing or rejecting a Plan does not necessarily alter the Intention from which it arose. Conversely, abandoning an Intention may invalidate Plans that depend upon it.

Plans are instruments of Intention. Intentions do not exist merely to justify Plans.

## Expression and Provenance

An Intention may be:

- explicitly declared by the Person;
- captured from a statement or note;
- imported from another system;
- derived from a Commitment;
- suggested by Parallel You;
- or inferred from observed behaviour.

The source of an Intention forms part of its meaning.

Parallel You shall distinguish between an Intention expressed by the Person and one inferred or proposed by the system.

A suggested Intention does not become the Person’s Intention until the Person accepts it or otherwise provides sufficient evidence of adoption.

Repeated behaviour may suggest an Intention, but behaviour alone does not conclusively establish one.

## Scope

An Intention may apply to:

- a single moment;
- a defined interval;
- a Context;
- a Project;
- a relationship;
- a role;
- a recurring situation;
- or the Person’s life generally.

Scope may be explicit, inferred, or unknown.

The system shall not apply an Intention outside its known scope without sufficient reason.

## Specificity

Intentions may exist at different levels of specificity.

Examples include:

- maintain my health;
- improve my sleep;
- establish a consistent evening routine;
- stop working by 9:00 p.m. tonight.

A specific Intention may refine or support a broader Intention without replacing it.

Parallel You may help clarify an Intention when clarification would improve planning. It shall not demand precision before the Person possesses it.

Ambiguity may be an accurate representation of the Person’s present understanding.

## Conditions

An Intention may depend upon conditions.

Conditions may concern:

- time;
- place;
- available energy;
- available attention;
- another Person;
- external events;
- completion of another Activity;
- access to a resource;
- or the continued relevance of the Intention itself.

A conditional Intention shall not be treated as immediately actionable when its conditions are not satisfied.

Unknown conditions should remain unknown rather than being presumed satisfied.

## Relative Importance

Intentions may differ in importance, urgency, persistence, and confidence.

These properties are related but distinct.

An important Intention may not be urgent.

An urgent Intention may not be important.

A persistent Intention may be weakly held.

A strongly expressed Intention may still be uncertain.

Parallel You may represent these differences, but it shall avoid reducing them to a single universal priority value where doing so would conceal meaningful distinctions.

## Conflict

A Person may possess Intentions that compete or conflict.

Conflict may arise because Intentions require:

- the same time;
- incompatible actions;
- limited energy or attention;
- contradictory outcomes;
- or choices among competing values and responsibilities.

The existence of conflict does not make an Intention invalid.

Parallel You should make relevant conflicts visible and help the Person consider alternatives. It shall not silently resolve conflicts by discarding, demoting, or rewriting an Intention.

## Lifecycle

An Intention may be:

- captured;
- clarified;
- adopted;
- active;
- deferred;
- satisfied;
- abandoned;
- superseded;
- or no longer relevant.

Lifecycle descriptions represent the system’s understanding of the Intention. They do not determine the Person’s internal state.

An Intention may return after being deferred or abandoned.

Satisfaction may be partial, temporary, recurring, or uncertain.

Abandonment is a valid outcome and shall not be represented as failure.

## Revision

The Person may revise an Intention as understanding and circumstances change.

Revision may alter:

- meaning;
- scope;
- conditions;
- importance;
- expected outcome;
- related Activities;
- or the Person’s willingness to pursue it.

Where continuity matters, Parallel You should preserve that revision occurred without pretending that the earlier and later Intentions are identical in every respect.

A materially changed Intention may supersede an earlier Intention rather than overwrite it.

## Relationships

An Intention may:

- belong to a Person;
- arise within a Context;
- express or support a Value;
- contain or relate to a Goal;
- become or produce a Commitment;
- be served by one or more Activities;
- guide one or more Plans;
- depend upon Conditions;
- conflict with other Intentions;
- and change through revision or lifecycle transition.

These relationships assist planning. They do not exhaust the meaning of the Intention.

## Principles

- Intentions provide direction without necessarily prescribing action.
- An Intention may be meaningful before it is precise.
- Not every Intention should become a Goal, Activity, Commitment, or Plan.
- The source and certainty of an Intention remain relevant.
- Suggested and inferred Intentions remain distinguishable from adopted Intentions.
- Conflicting Intentions may coexist.
- Intentions may change without implying inconsistency or failure.
- Abandonment is a legitimate exercise of agency.
- Plans and Activities serve Intentions, not the reverse.

## Invariants

Every implementation of Parallel You shall preserve the following invariants:

- An Intention shall not be treated as equivalent to a Desire, Goal, Activity, Commitment, or Plan.
- An inferred or suggested Intention shall not be represented as explicitly declared by the Person.
- Repeated behaviour shall not, by itself, conclusively establish an Intention.
- Capture shall not require an Intention to be fully clarified.
- Ambiguity shall not be silently replaced with invented precision.
- Importance, urgency, persistence, and confidence shall not be presumed equivalent.
- A conflict between Intentions shall not silently invalidate either Intention.
- Changing a Plan shall not necessarily change the Intention it serves.
- Failure to perform a related Activity shall not, by itself, invalidate the Intention.
- Deferral, revision, satisfaction, abandonment, and supersession shall remain distinguishable.
- An abandoned Intention shall not be treated as a moral or operational failure.
- The Person shall retain authority to adopt, revise, defer, or abandon an Intention.
