# Energy

## Definition

**Energy** is the Person’s variable capacity to undertake and sustain Activity.

Energy describes whether and to what degree the Person is presently able to meet the demands of an Activity without reference to Time alone.

Energy is represented operationally rather than physiologically. Parallel You does not claim to measure biological energy directly. It maintains a contextual model derived from the Person’s experience, available observations, and planning needs.

## Purpose

The Energy concept allows Parallel You to:

- distinguish available Time from usable capacity;
- represent the demands of Activities;
- identify Activities suited to the Person’s present condition;
- anticipate depletion and recovery;
- construct more realistic Plans;
- recognize patterns across Activity Occurrences;
- and revise future recommendations using observed experience.

The purpose of representing Energy is not to extract maximum effort.

Its purpose is to help the Person use, preserve, and restore capacity deliberately.

## Energy and Time

Energy and Time are related but distinct.

A Person may have Time without sufficient Energy for a particular Activity.

A Person may have Energy while lacking enough uninterrupted Time.

An Activity may fit within an available Interval while exceeding the Person’s sustainable capacity.

Parallel You shall not infer Energy from Calendar availability alone.

Increasing the Time assigned to an Activity does not necessarily make the Activity more achievable. In some circumstances, a longer Duration may increase demand, permit a slower pace, or provide needed recovery. The relationship is contextual.

## Energy and Attention

Energy and Attention are related but distinct.

A Person may possess Energy while lacking the ability to direct or sustain Attention.

A Person may sustain Attention for a limited period despite low Energy, sometimes at a later cost.

An Activity may require substantial physical Energy with little sustained Attention, or substantial Attention with little physical exertion.

Parallel You shall not use Energy as a substitute for representing Attention.

## Energy and Motivation

Energy is not Motivation.

A Person may have the capacity to perform an Activity without wanting to do it.

A Person may strongly want to perform an Activity while lacking the capacity to begin or sustain it.

Reluctance, avoidance, uncertainty, and lack of interest shall not automatically be interpreted as low Energy.

Likewise, enthusiasm shall not be treated as proof of sufficient Energy.

## Energy and Mood

Energy is not Mood.

Mood may influence experienced Energy, Activity suitability, and willingness to act, but the concepts remain distinct.

A Person may feel emotionally distressed while possessing substantial physical or cognitive capacity. They may feel content while physically exhausted.

Parallel You shall not infer emotional state solely from an Energy report.

## Energy and Health

Energy may be affected by health, sleep, pain, medication, nutrition, stress, disability, illness, and other bodily or psychological conditions.

Parallel You may represent relevant observations when the Person chooses to provide them.

The system shall not diagnose health conditions from Energy patterns.

An Energy model is a planning aid, not a medical assessment.

## Dimensions

Energy may possess multiple dimensions.

Useful dimensions may include:

- physical;
- cognitive;
- emotional;
- social;
- creative;
- executive;
- and other Person-defined forms of capacity.

These dimensions are not presumed universal or independent.

A Person may experience Energy in ways that do not fit a predefined taxonomy. Parallel You should permit the model to remain simple or evolve according to demonstrated value.

The system shall not require the Person to classify Energy more precisely than they find useful.

## Energy State

An **Energy State** represents the system’s understanding of the Person’s Energy within a particular temporal and contextual scope.

An Energy State may be:

- explicitly reported;
- observed indirectly;
- imported from another source;
- inferred;
- forecast;
- or unknown.

An Energy State may include:

- one or more dimensions;
- an approximate level or range;
- temporal scope;
- Context;
- confidence;
- source;
- and relevant conditions.

An Energy State is a provisional representation, not a fixed property of the Person.

## Available Energy

**Available Energy** is the capacity the Person may presently draw upon for Activity.

Available Energy is Activity-dependent.

The same Person in the same moment may have sufficient Energy for one Activity and insufficient Energy for another.

Available Energy may also depend upon:

- expected Duration;
- pace;
- environment;
- support;
- familiarity;
- interruption risk;
- consequences;
- and the need to preserve capacity for later Commitments.

Parallel You shall not treat Energy as a single universally spendable quantity unless that simplification is useful and explicitly understood as approximate.

## Reserve

An **Energy Reserve** is capacity intentionally or implicitly preserved for future need, uncertainty, recovery, or resilience.

Reserve may be needed for:

- essential self-care;
- family responsibilities;
- expected Commitments;
- travel;
- emergencies;
- transition;
- or the ordinary variability of life.

Available Energy shall not automatically be treated as expendable Energy.

Parallel You should avoid Plans that consume all estimated capacity merely because that capacity appears available.

## Activity Demand

**Energy Demand** is the capacity an Activity is expected to require from the Person.

Demand may differ across Energy dimensions.

An Activity may be:

- physically demanding;
- cognitively demanding;
- emotionally demanding;
- socially demanding;
- restorative in one dimension;
- and depleting in another.

Demand is contextual rather than intrinsic.

The same Activity may require different Energy depending upon:

- the Person’s familiarity;
- complexity;
- quality expectations;
- available tools;
- social conditions;
- environment;
- uncertainty;
- Duration;
- and current Energy State.

An estimated Energy Demand is a planning hypothesis.

## Compatibility

**Energy Compatibility** describes how well the Person’s Energy State matches the demands of an Activity under present conditions.

Compatibility is not determined solely by comparing two numerical levels.

An Activity with modest total demand may still be unsuitable if it requires a form of Energy the Person does not presently possess.

An Activity with high demand may be suitable when it is restorative, compelling, well-supported, or aligned with the Person’s current Energy.

Parallel You should recommend Activities based upon compatibility rather than presumed productivity value alone.

## Expenditure

**Energy Expenditure** describes capacity consumed, redirected, or made less available through Activity.

Expenditure may occur:

- during an Activity;
- through preparation or anticipation;
- during transition;
- through interruption;
- after an Activity;
- or through conditions unrelated to deliberate Activity.

Energy Expenditure is not necessarily proportional to Duration.

A brief Activity may be intensely demanding. A prolonged Activity may be sustainable or restorative.

Parallel You shall not infer expenditure solely from elapsed Time.

## Recovery

**Recovery** is a process through which capacity becomes available again.

Recovery may involve:

- sleep;
- rest;
- food;
- hydration;
- solitude;
- movement;
- recreation;
- social connection;
- completion of a stressful obligation;
- change of Context;
- or other Person-specific conditions.

Recovery is itself a legitimate purpose for Activity and Time.

Parallel You shall not treat recovery merely as maintenance required to enable further productivity.

Recovery patterns may be uncertain and variable. The system should learn from the Person’s experience without prescribing a universal model.

## Restorative Activity

A **Restorative Activity** is an Activity that may preserve or increase capacity in one or more Energy dimensions.

An Activity may be simultaneously restorative and demanding.

For example, exercise may consume physical Energy while improving mood or later capacity. Social contact may restore one Person and deplete another, or affect the same Person differently under different conditions.

Parallel You shall not maintain a universal classification of restorative Activities independent of the Person and Context.

## Depletion

**Depletion** is a condition in which available Energy is materially reduced relative to present or anticipated demand.

Depletion may be local to an Energy dimension or Activity type.

A depleted Person may still be capable of Activity, but continued action may require disproportionate effort, reduce quality, increase risk, or consume future capacity.

Parallel You shall not interpret the ability to continue as proof that continued Activity is sustainable.

## Sustainability

**Sustainability** describes whether an Activity, Plan, or pattern of demand can continue without unacceptable depletion or consequence.

A Plan may be feasible once while remaining unsustainable when repeated.

Parallel You should consider both immediate capacity and anticipated effects upon future capacity.

The Person determines which costs and consequences are acceptable.

The system may warn, but it shall not silently substitute its own risk tolerance.

## Momentum and Activation

Beginning an Activity may require a distinct form of Energy or effort.

**Activation Cost** is the demand associated with initiating or resuming an Activity.

**Momentum** is a condition in which continuing an Activity may require less effort than beginning or switching away from it.

Activation Cost and Momentum may be influenced by:

- clarity;
- familiarity;
- emotional resistance;
- setup;
- environment;
- interruption;
- and current Attention.

Parallel You should account for the cost of starting and switching Activities rather than considering only their sustained demand.

## Transition Cost

A change between Activities, Contexts, environments, or modes of engagement may consume Energy.

Transition Cost may arise from:

- physical movement;
- setup and cleanup;
- emotional adjustment;
- decision-making;
- reconstruction of context;
- disengagement;
- and reorientation of Attention.

Transition Cost may remain significant even when little Calendar Time is consumed.

Parallel You shall not assume that several individually suitable Activities remain suitable when combined through frequent transitions.

## Forecasting

An **Energy Forecast** estimates future Energy State.

A Forecast may consider:

- current Energy;
- expected Activity;
- Time of day;
- sleep or recovery;
- recurring patterns;
- known health factors;
- environmental conditions;
- and prior Observations.

Forecasts are inherently uncertain.

Past patterns may inform future expectations but shall not be treated as destiny.

Parallel You shall expose relevant uncertainty and permit the Person to override or correct a Forecast.

## Observation

An **Energy Observation** records evidence concerning Energy State, Activity Demand, Expenditure, Recovery, or suitability.

Observation may be obtained through:

- a direct check-in;
- retrospective reflection;
- Activity outcomes;
- behavioural signals;
- a device;
- an external service;
- or inference.

Different sources may disagree.

The Person’s report possesses special authority concerning their experienced Energy, but it may still be approximate, contextual, or revised later.

Parallel You shall preserve source and confidence where they materially affect interpretation.

## Scale and Measurement

Energy may be represented using:

- qualitative descriptions;
- ordinal levels;
- ranges;
- Person-defined categories;
- multidimensional profiles;
- or other operational scales.

An Energy value is not a physical measurement unless it originates from an appropriate physical measure and is represented as such.

A numerical scale may support comparison without implying objective units or linear relationships.

The difference between levels two and three shall not automatically be presumed equal to the difference between levels three and four.

Precision shall not exceed the Person’s ability or willingness to make the distinction.

## Variability

Energy varies across Time, Context, Activity, and circumstance.

Variation is ordinary.

Parallel You should distinguish persistent patterns from temporary conditions and preserve the possibility that either may change.

An unusual Energy State shall not automatically be treated as noise merely because it differs from historical patterns.

## Planning

An Energy-aware Plan should consider:

- the compatibility of Activities with expected Energy;
- Energy Reserve;
- transition and activation costs;
- recovery;
- uncertainty;
- essential Commitments;
- and the cumulative sustainability of demand.

Parallel You shall not schedule every demanding Activity into the Person’s predicted highest-energy intervals without considering competition among Intentions and the need for resilience.

Energy-aware planning should expand practical choice, not become another mechanism for controlling the Person.

## Relationships

Energy may:

- constrain or enable Activities;
- vary across Time and Context;
- interact with Attention, Mood, Motivation, and Health;
- be consumed through Activity and transition;
- be restored through recovery;
- influence Plans and Recommendations;
- and be estimated through Observations and historical patterns.

These relationships support planning. They do not define the Person’s character, diligence, or worth.

## Principles

- Energy is finite, variable, contextual, and Activity-dependent.
- Energy is distinct from Time, Attention, Motivation, Mood, and Health.
- Available Energy is not necessarily expendable Energy.
- Activity Demand is contextual rather than intrinsic.
- Energy may possess multiple dimensions without requiring a universal taxonomy.
- Recovery and reserve are legitimate planning concerns.
- Capacity to continue does not prove sustainability.
- Numerical representation does not imply objective or linear measurement.
- Energy forecasts are uncertain and revisable.
- The purpose of Energy-aware planning is sustainable agency, not maximum extraction.

## Invariants

Every implementation of Parallel You shall preserve the following invariants:

- Available Time shall not be treated as proof of available Energy.
- Energy shall not be treated as equivalent to Attention, Motivation, Mood, health, willingness, or moral character.
- An Energy State shall not be treated as a fixed property of the Person.
- An inferred Energy State shall remain distinguishable from one reported by the Person.
- Energy Demand shall not be treated as universal or immutable for an Activity.
- Elapsed Time shall not be treated as a sufficient measure of Energy Expenditure.
- Available Energy shall not automatically be treated as expendable capacity.
- The ability to perform an Activity shall not be treated as proof that the Activity is sustainable.
- Recovery shall not be treated solely as preparation for further productivity.
- A Restorative Activity shall not be classified independently of Person and Context where those distinctions matter.
- Energy dimensions shall not be imposed when they create more burden than planning value.
- Numerical Energy scales shall not imply unsupported physical units, precision, or linearity.
- Forecasts shall not be represented with greater certainty than their evidence supports.
- Historical Energy patterns shall not be treated as immutable limits.
- Low Energy shall not be represented as failure, laziness, or insufficient discipline.
- Energy-aware planning shall preserve the Person’s authority to choose how capacity is used.
