# Attention

## Definition

**Attention** is the Person’s limited and variable capacity to select, orient toward, and remain engaged with information, Activity, or experience.

Attention determines what receives effective mental priority within a particular moment or Interval.

Parallel You represents Attention operationally so that it may reason about focus, interruption, switching, continuity, and the suitability of Activities.

Attention is not a measure of the Person’s care, character, intelligence, or commitment.

## Purpose

The Attention concept allows Parallel You to:

- distinguish available Time and Energy from usable focus;
- represent the attentional demands of Activities;
- account for interruption and switching;
- preserve continuity across pauses;
- identify suitable Activities under present conditions;
- reduce unnecessary cognitive administration;
- and construct Plans that respect the limits of sustained engagement.

The purpose of representing Attention is not to enforce continuous focus.

Its purpose is to help the Person direct limited awareness without requiring them to remember and coordinate everything at once.

## Attention and Time

Attention occurs within Time, but available Time does not imply available Attention.

A Person may possess an open Interval while being unable to sustain engagement with a demanding Activity.

An Activity may require uninterrupted Attention even when its total Duration is brief.

A longer Interval may permit deeper engagement, but it may also exceed the Person’s sustainable attentional capacity.

Parallel You shall not infer Attention from Calendar availability alone.

## Attention and Energy

Attention and Energy are related but distinct.

A Person may possess substantial Energy while experiencing fragmented or unavailable Attention.

A Person may sustain focused Attention temporarily despite low Energy, sometimes at a later cost.

Energy may affect the ability to direct, sustain, recover, or shift Attention, but it does not fully determine those capacities.

Parallel You shall represent attentional suitability independently where the distinction provides planning value.

## Attention and Intention

Attention is not Intention.

A Person may intend to perform an Activity while their Attention is repeatedly drawn elsewhere.

A Person may attend to something without intending to prioritize, continue, or value it.

The present object of Attention shall not be treated as conclusive evidence of the Person’s Intentions.

Likewise, failure to attend to an Intention shall not be treated as evidence that the Intention is insincere or unimportant.

## Attention and Priority

Attention is not Priority.

An important Activity may receive little Attention because it is difficult to begin, poorly defined, emotionally demanding, frequently interrupted, or incompatible with the Person’s present state.

A low-priority stimulus may capture Attention through novelty, urgency, discomfort, or environmental design.

Parallel You shall not infer importance from observed Attention alone.

## Attention and Motivation

Attention is not Motivation.

A Person may want to perform an Activity while being unable to orient toward or remain engaged with it.

A Person may become deeply engaged in an Activity they did not initially want to perform.

Motivation may influence Attention, but neither concept determines the other.

## Attention State

An **Attention State** represents the system’s understanding of the Person’s present or anticipated attentional capacity.

An Attention State may describe:

- availability;
- stability;
- depth;
- selectivity;
- flexibility;
- fragmentation;
- susceptibility to interruption;
- and ease of orientation or switching.

An Attention State may be:

- explicitly reported;
- observed indirectly;
- imported;
- inferred;
- forecast;
- or unknown.

It is a contextual and revisable representation, not a fixed property of the Person.

## Attention Modes

Attention may operate in different modes.

Useful modes may include:

- sustained focus;
- scanning;
- monitoring;
- conversation;
- reactive response;
- diffuse thought;
- reflection;
- exploration;
- and recovery.

These modes are not necessarily mutually exclusive or universally experienced.

No mode is inherently superior.

Diffuse, exploratory, and receptive Attention may be valuable even when they do not produce immediate measurable output.

Parallel You shall not define focused production as the only legitimate use of Attention.

## Attention Demand

**Attention Demand** is the kind and degree of Attention an Activity is expected to require.

Demand may concern:

- depth;
- continuity;
- precision;
- responsiveness;
- working memory;
- decision-making;
- sensory monitoring;
- social interpretation;
- or resistance to interruption.

Attention Demand is contextual.

The same Activity may require different Attention depending upon:

- familiarity;
- complexity;
- available tools;
- quality expectations;
- environment;
- emotional significance;
- interruption;
- and the state in which the Activity is resumed.

An estimated Attention Demand is a planning hypothesis.

## Attention Compatibility

**Attention Compatibility** describes how well the Person’s Attention State matches the demands of an Activity under present conditions.

Compatibility may depend upon:

- available uninterrupted Time;
- Energy;
- environment;
- urgency;
- activation cost;
- existing momentum;
- expected interruptions;
- and the consequences of error.

An Activity with low total demand may still be incompatible if it requires a kind of Attention the Person cannot presently provide.

Parallel You should recommend Activities based upon compatibility rather than importance or available Time alone.

## Orientation

**Orientation** is the process of directing Attention toward an Activity, Context, stimulus, or concern.

Orientation may require:

- remembering what was being done;
- locating relevant information;
- reconstructing context;
- selecting a next action;
- suppressing competing demands;
- and preparing the environment.

Orientation imposes a cost.

Parallel You should reduce this cost by preserving context and presenting a clear point of entry when the Person begins or resumes an Activity.

## Sustained Attention

**Sustained Attention** is the continued direction of Attention toward an Activity or purpose over an Interval.

Sustained Attention may vary in depth and stability.

Momentary lapses, environmental awareness, and internal thought do not necessarily mean that sustained Attention has ended.

A timer or active application window does not prove sustained Attention.

Parallel You shall not represent uninterrupted focus with greater certainty than available evidence supports.

## Interruption

An **Interruption** is an Event or change that displaces, divides, or materially alters the Person’s Attention during an Activity.

An Interruption may be:

- external or internal;
- expected or unexpected;
- voluntary or imposed;
- relevant or unrelated;
- brief or extended;
- and beneficial or disruptive.

An Interruption may end an Activity Occurrence, pause it, or merely alter the quality of engagement.

Parallel You shall not assume that every Interruption is harmful or that every interrupted Activity should be resumed.

## Distraction

A **Distraction** is a stimulus, thought, Event, or competing Activity that draws Attention away from its present or intended object.

Distraction is contextual.

Something is not a Distraction merely because it differs from the current Plan. It may represent:

- a legitimate competing Intention;
- an urgent Event;
- a needed recovery signal;
- relevant new information;
- spontaneous opportunity;
- or avoidance.

Parallel You should avoid classifying Attention shifts as distractions without sufficient context.

## Switching

**Attention Switching** is the redirection of Attention from one Activity, Context, or object to another.

Switching may be deliberate or reactive.

A switch may require:

- disengagement;
- preservation of current context;
- orientation toward the next Activity;
- reconstruction of prior knowledge;
- and later recovery of the original Activity.

Switching cost varies according to the Activities involved, their similarity, the Person’s state, and the quality of preserved context.

Parallel You shall not assume that switching is costless merely because it occurs quickly.

## Attention Residue

**Attention Residue** is the continued cognitive or emotional influence of a previous Activity after the Person has switched away from it.

Residue may arise from:

- unresolved decisions;
- incomplete thoughts;
- emotional activation;
- uncertainty;
- urgency;
- or fear of forgetting.

Attention Residue may reduce compatibility with the next Activity even when the previous Activity has formally ended.

Capturing state, decisions, and next actions may reduce residue by allowing the Person to disengage with confidence.

## Fragmentation

**Attention Fragmentation** is a condition in which Attention is repeatedly divided, redirected, or prevented from stabilizing.

Fragmentation may result from:

- frequent interruptions;
- notifications;
- multiple simultaneous responsibilities;
- environmental demands;
- unresolved concerns;
- excessive task switching;
- or the need to monitor several sources.

Fragmentation is not equivalent to lack of effort.

A fragmented Interval may remain useful for Activities with compatible attentional demands.

Parallel You should adapt recommendations to fragmentation rather than demanding a state of focus the current environment cannot support.

## Concurrency

A Person may participate in more than one Activity during the same Interval, but Attention is not thereby multiplied.

Apparent concurrency may involve:

- rapid switching;
- background monitoring;
- an automated or low-attention Activity;
- shared sensory input;
- or imprecise observation.

Parallel You shall not presume equal or continuous Attention across concurrent Activities.

An Activity may continue in Time while receiving little or no active Attention.

## Activation

**Attentional Activation** is the process of beginning meaningful engagement with an Activity.

Activation may require more effort than sustaining an Activity once engagement has formed.

Activation cost may be influenced by:

- ambiguity;
- emotional resistance;
- missing context;
- environmental friction;
- expected difficulty;
- interruption risk;
- and the number of decisions required before action can begin.

Parallel You should reduce activation cost by helping identify a clear and suitably small point of entry.

Difficulty beginning shall not be treated as evidence that the Activity or Intention lacks importance.

## Momentum

**Attentional Momentum** is a condition in which continued engagement becomes easier or more natural than disengagement or switching.

Momentum may improve depth, speed, continuity, or satisfaction.

Momentum may also cause the Person to continue beyond sustainable Energy, available Time, or other Commitments.

Parallel You should preserve beneficial Momentum where practical while making relevant constraints visible.

It shall not interrupt merely to preserve a previously generated Schedule when continuing better serves the Person.

## Flow

**Flow** is a state of deep and sustained engagement in which Activity, Attention, and perceived capability are closely aligned.

Flow may alter the Person’s experience of Time, effort, and self-consciousness.

Flow can be valuable, but it is not required for meaningful Activity.

Parallel You shall not treat Flow as a universal objective or interpret its absence as deficient performance.

## Recovery

Attention may require recovery.

Attentional recovery may involve:

- rest;
- sleep;
- movement;
- reduced stimulation;
- change of Activity;
- unstructured thought;
- recreation;
- completion of unresolved concerns;
- or environmental change.

Recovery is a legitimate use of Time.

Parallel You shall not treat every pause in directed Attention as an opportunity to insert another Activity.

## Externalization

**Externalization** is the transfer of information from active memory or internal rehearsal into a reliable external representation.

Externalization may include recording:

- an Intention;
- a next action;
- an unresolved question;
- a decision;
- current progress;
- or the context needed to resume.

Parallel You should use externalization to reduce the burden of remembering and to support confident disengagement.

The cost of externalization shall remain lower than the burden it removes.

## Protection

An **Attention Protection** is a condition or boundary intended to reduce interruption, fragmentation, or switching during an Activity.

Protection may include:

- reserved Time;
- notification suppression;
- environmental change;
- communication of unavailability;
- preparation of required resources;
- or deferral of unrelated decisions.

Protection should be proportional to the Activity’s demand and the Person’s circumstances.

Parallel You shall not presume authority to block communication or alter external systems without appropriate delegation.

## Observation

An **Attention Observation** records evidence concerning orientation, engagement, interruption, switching, fragmentation, or recovery.

Observation may come from:

- direct report;
- interaction history;
- timers;
- device activity;
- application state;
- environmental signals;
- or inference.

These sources provide incomplete evidence.

Application focus does not prove human Attention.

Lack of interaction does not prove lack of Attention.

Parallel You shall preserve source, confidence, and uncertainty where they materially affect interpretation.

## Measurement

Attention may be represented through:

- qualitative descriptions;
- Person-defined modes;
- interruption counts;
- approximate continuity;
- observed switching;
- self-reported depth;
- or other operational measures.

No single measure establishes the quality or value of Attention.

Numerical representation shall not imply objective precision where none exists.

The system shall not demand frequent Attention reporting when the reporting itself would materially disrupt Attention.

## Planning

An Attention-aware Plan should consider:

- Activity demand;
- available uninterrupted Intervals;
- activation and switching costs;
- Attention Residue;
- likely interruptions;
- current Momentum;
- environmental conditions;
- Energy;
- and recovery.

Activities requiring similar Context may be grouped when doing so reduces switching cost.

Activities requiring deep or sustained Attention should not consume every apparently suitable Interval without allowance for recovery, uncertainty, and other forms of life.

The purpose of Attention-aware planning is to make engagement easier and more deliberate, not to maintain continuous concentration.

## Relationships

Attention may:

- be directed toward Activities, Events, thoughts, or sensory experience;
- vary across Time, Context, and environment;
- interact with Energy, Motivation, Mood, and Health;
- be displaced by Interruption or Distraction;
- incur costs through orientation and switching;
- persist through Momentum and Residue;
- recover through rest or change;
- and influence Plans and Recommendations.

These relationships support planning. They do not define the Person’s diligence, sincerity, or worth.

## Principles

- Attention is finite, variable, contextual, and selective.
- Attention is distinct from Time, Energy, Intention, Priority, Motivation, and Mood.
- Focus is one legitimate mode of Attention, not its universal ideal.
- Orientation, switching, interruption, and resumption impose real costs.
- Apparent concurrency does not multiply Attention.
- Difficulty attending does not establish lack of care or importance.
- Externalization should reduce the burden of remembering.
- Attention Observations remain incomplete and uncertain.
- Recovery and unstructured Attention are legitimate.
- The purpose of Attention-aware planning is agency and continuity, not constant focus.

## Invariants

Every implementation of Parallel You shall preserve the following invariants:

- Available Time shall not be treated as proof of available Attention.
- Available Energy shall not be treated as proof of available Attention.
- Attention shall not be treated as equivalent to Intention, Priority, Motivation, Mood, intelligence, discipline, or moral character.
- Observed Attention shall not conclusively establish what the Person values or intends.
- Failure to attend shall not, by itself, invalidate an Intention or Commitment.
- Application state, device activity, or timer state shall not be treated as proof of human Attention.
- Overlapping Activities shall not be presumed to receive equal or simultaneous Attention.
- Switching shall not be treated as costless.
- An Interruption shall not automatically be classified as harmful.
- A shift from the Plan shall not automatically be classified as Distraction.
- Focus shall not be treated as the only valuable Attention mode.
- Flow shall not be required for meaningful or successful Activity.
- Attention recovery shall not be treated as wasted Time.
- Attention reporting shall not impose greater disruption than the planning value it provides.
- Inferred Attention States shall remain distinguishable from those reported by the Person.
- Attention-aware planning shall preserve the Person’s authority to decide what receives their Attention.
