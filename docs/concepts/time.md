# Time

## Definition

**Time** is the temporal context within which Intentions, Commitments, Activities, Events, Plans, and Observations are situated.

Parallel You represents Time so that it may reason about:

- when something may, should, or did occur;
- how long something may require or did require;
- the order in which things occur;
- the intervals available to the Person;
- and the temporal constraints affecting action.

Time as represented by the system is a model. It is not identical to the Person’s lived experience of Time.

## Purpose

The Time concept allows Parallel You to:

- represent temporal constraints;
- understand availability;
- estimate and observe duration;
- arrange Activities;
- identify conflicts;
- account for transitions and uncertainty;
- support recurrence;
- and revise Plans as Time passes.

Its purpose is not to fill every interval.

Its purpose is to help the Person use temporal information in service of their Intentions and Commitments.

## Finite Time

A Person has finite Time.

Time cannot be accumulated, recovered, or used more than once. Nevertheless, the same interval may serve several purposes, contain overlapping Activities, or possess value without being assigned to a planned Activity.

Parallel You shall not treat all unscheduled Time as unused capacity.

An interval may be needed for:

- rest;
- transition;
- recovery;
- uncertainty;
- spontaneous action;
- environmental demands;
- or the ordinary maintenance of life.

Unallocated Time is not necessarily available Time.

Available Time is not necessarily usable capacity.

## Instant

An **Instant** is a particular position in Time.

An Instant may represent:

- the beginning or end of an Activity;
- a Deadline;
- the occurrence of an Event;
- the creation of a record;
- or the moment at which an Observation was made.

Recorded Instants may be exact, approximate, inferred, rounded, or unknown.

Parallel You shall preserve relevant uncertainty rather than presenting every timestamp as exact.

## Interval

An **Interval** is a span between temporal boundaries.

An Interval may have:

- a known beginning and end;
- a known beginning and unknown end;
- an unknown beginning and known end;
- an estimated duration;
- or approximate boundaries.

Intervals may overlap, contain other Intervals, or remain disjoint.

An Interval may describe:

- availability;
- an Activity Occurrence;
- an Event;
- a Plan horizon;
- a period of rest;
- a recurring window;
- or another temporally bounded condition.

## Duration

A **Duration** is the amount of Time between two temporal boundaries or the estimated amount of Time associated with something.

Duration may be:

- observed;
- estimated;
- required;
- preferred;
- minimum;
- maximum;
- expected;
- or uncertain.

An observed Duration describes available evidence concerning what occurred.

An estimated Duration is a planning hypothesis.

The Duration of one Activity Occurrence shall not be presumed to define every future Occurrence of the same Activity.

## Sequence

Temporal sequence describes the order in which Instants, Events, or Activities occur.

Sequence may be known even when exact timestamps are not.

An Activity may need to occur:

- before another Activity;
- after an Event;
- during an Interval;
- concurrently with another Activity;
- or in no particular order.

Parallel You shall preserve the distinction between a temporal ordering constraint and a dependency.

Something occurring first does not necessarily cause or enable what follows.

## Temporal Reference

A temporal representation depends upon a reference system.

Relevant reference information may include:

- date;
- local time;
- time zone;
- offset from coordinated time;
- locale;
- calendar;
- and daylight-saving rules.

Parallel You shall preserve enough reference information to interpret Time correctly.

A local time without a date or time zone may be ambiguous.

A future local time may change its coordinated-time offset because of daylight-saving or legal changes.

The system shall not silently reinterpret a recorded Instant merely because the Person’s current location or time zone changes.

## Calendar Time

**Calendar Time** represents Time through human conventions such as dates, days, weeks, months, and years.

Calendar units do not always possess fixed Durations.

A month is not a fixed number of days.

A local day may not contain exactly twenty-four hours.

Parallel You shall distinguish calendar arithmetic from fixed-duration arithmetic where the difference affects planning or recurrence.

## Availability

**Availability** is an Interval during which the Person may be able or willing to undertake Activity.

Availability may be:

- explicitly declared;
- derived from a calendar;
- inferred from routine;
- imported from another system;
- conditional;
- or unknown.

Availability is not equivalent to unoccupied Calendar Time.

An apparently open Interval may still be unsuitable because of:

- insufficient Energy;
- insufficient Attention;
- location;
- transition;
- recovery;
- responsibility;
- uncertainty;
- or another unrecorded condition.

Parallel You shall treat Availability as evidence of possibility, not proof of capacity.

## Allocation

An **Allocation** associates some portion of Time with an Intention, Commitment, Activity, Context, or purpose.

Allocation may be:

- planned;
- reserved;
- tentative;
- observed;
- or retrospectively assigned.

Allocation does not create Time.

Allocating the same Interval more than once may represent:

- a conflict;
- concurrency;
- conditional alternatives;
- imprecise records;
- or multiple purposes served by one Activity.

Parallel You shall not assume that every overlapping Allocation is erroneous.

## Schedule

A **Schedule** is a temporal arrangement of Activities, Events, Commitments, or reserved Intervals.

A Schedule may form part of a Plan, but it is not the whole Plan.

A scheduled Activity represents an intention or expectation concerning Time. It does not establish that the Activity occurred.

Schedules may include:

- fixed appointments;
- flexible work;
- tentative reservations;
- protected Time;
- transitions;
- buffers;
- and unallocated Intervals.

Parallel You shall preserve the flexibility and authority of scheduled items where known.

## Temporal Constraint

A **Temporal Constraint** limits when an Activity, Commitment, Event, or Outcome may occur.

Temporal Constraints may include:

- a Deadline;
- a release time;
- an earliest start;
- a latest start;
- a fixed appointment;
- an availability window;
- an ordering requirement;
- a minimum separation;
- or a maximum delay.

A Temporal Constraint may be:

- hard or flexible;
- explicit or inferred;
- externally imposed or self-selected;
- permanent or provisional.

The system shall preserve the source and flexibility of the constraint.

## Deadline

A **Deadline** is a temporal boundary associated with an expected Activity, Outcome, Commitment, or consequence.

A Deadline may indicate:

- when something must be completed;
- when an opportunity ends;
- when a response is expected;
- or when consequences may begin.

A Deadline is not equivalent to a scheduled work interval.

A Person may need one or more earlier Activities to satisfy a Deadline.

Parallel You should reason backward from relevant Deadlines without representing every intermediate planning target as equally binding.

Passing a Deadline does not necessarily end the associated Commitment or Activity.

## Flexibility

Temporal arrangements differ in flexibility.

An Activity may be:

- fixed to a particular Interval;
- movable within a window;
- divisible across several Intervals;
- interruptible;
- deferrable;
- conditional;
- or unscheduled.

Flexibility may be limited by external conditions, other Parties, Activity requirements, or the Person’s preference.

Parallel You shall not move an Activity merely because doing so is technically possible.

A movable Activity may still carry transition, coordination, or emotional costs.

## Transition

A **Transition** is the Time and effort required to move between Activities, locations, Contexts, or states of Attention.

Transition may include:

- travel;
- setup;
- cleanup;
- preparation;
- decompression;
- context reconstruction;
- and recovery from interruption.

Transition Time is part of the Person’s temporal reality.

Parallel You shall not construct Plans that assume instantaneous movement between incompatible Activities or Contexts.

## Buffer

A **Buffer** is Time intentionally left available to absorb uncertainty, transition, delay, recovery, or unexpected demand.

A Buffer is not wasted Time.

Buffers increase the resilience of Plans.

Parallel You may recommend Buffers based on uncertainty and observed variation. It shall not automatically consume a Buffer merely because preceding Activities appear to fit within their estimates.

## Estimation

A temporal **Estimate** expresses uncertain knowledge concerning an Instant, Duration, Interval, or sequence.

An Estimate may include:

- a likely value;
- a range;
- a confidence level;
- a source;
- and assumptions.

Parallel You should improve Estimates using relevant Observations while preserving variation and context.

A more precise representation is not necessarily a more accurate one.

The system shall not manufacture exactness where available evidence supports only approximation.

## Observed Time

**Observed Time** represents evidence concerning when or for how long something occurred.

Observation may come from:

- direct entry;
- a timer;
- a calendar;
- a device;
- an external service;
- a contextual signal;
- or inference.

Different sources may disagree.

A timer measures elapsed Time under particular operational assumptions. It does not prove that the Person continuously performed or attended to the associated Activity.

Parallel You shall preserve the source and confidence of temporal Observations.

## Experienced Time

The Person’s experience of Time may differ from measured Duration.

An Activity may feel brief or prolonged. Time pressure, fatigue, interest, interruption, anxiety, and immersion may affect temporal experience.

Parallel You may record experienced Time where it provides planning value.

Experienced Time shall not replace measured Duration, and measured Duration shall not invalidate the Person’s experience.

## Recurrence

A **Recurrence** describes an expectation or pattern repeated according to temporal rules.

Recurrence may be based upon:

- fixed Durations;
- calendar positions;
- completed Occurrences;
- Events;
- conditions;
- or observed habits.

A recurring definition is distinct from each generated or observed Occurrence.

Changes to a recurring definition shall not silently rewrite historical Occurrences.

A missed Occurrence shall not automatically become accumulating temporal debt.

## Past, Present, and Future

Parallel You reasons differently about past, present, and future Time.

The past is represented through incomplete Observations and records.

The present is operationally immediate but may still be uncertain.

The future is represented through Intentions, Commitments, constraints, Estimates, and Plans.

Future Time shall not be represented with the certainty of recorded past Time.

Recorded past Time shall not be treated as perfectly known merely because it has already occurred.

## Relationships

Time may:

- constrain Intentions and Commitments;
- contain Activity Occurrences and Events;
- be allocated through Plans and Schedules;
- be estimated or observed;
- interact with Energy and Attention;
- include Transitions and Buffers;
- define recurrence;
- and provide evidence for reflection and future planning.

Temporal relationships assist planning. They do not determine the value of what occurs.

## Principles

- Time is finite, but not every Interval should be allocated.
- Available Time is not equivalent to available capacity.
- Unscheduled Time is not necessarily available Time.
- Instants, Intervals, Durations, and temporal constraints are distinct.
- Estimated Time is uncertain and revisable.
- Calendar Time and fixed Duration are not interchangeable.
- Transitions, Buffers, rest, and recovery occupy legitimate Time.
- Schedules describe expectations rather than facts.
- Temporal precision shall not exceed the available evidence.
- The purpose of temporal planning is resilience and agency, not maximum occupancy.

## Invariants

Every implementation of Parallel You shall preserve the following invariants:

- Time shall not be treated as the sole measure of capacity.
- An unallocated Interval shall not automatically be treated as available.
- Availability shall not be treated as proof that an Activity is suitable or possible.
- A scheduled Activity shall not be represented as an observed Activity.
- An estimated Duration shall not be represented as an observed Duration.
- A Deadline shall not be treated as equivalent to a scheduled work Interval.
- A self-selected target shall not be represented as an externally imposed Deadline.
- Calendar arithmetic shall not be silently replaced with fixed-duration arithmetic where the distinction matters.
- Temporal reference information shall not be discarded where doing so would make an Instant ambiguous.
- Transition and Buffer Time shall not be silently treated as waste.
- Overlapping Allocations shall not automatically be treated as either valid concurrency or scheduling error.
- A timer shall not be treated as proof of continuous Activity or Attention.
- A recurring definition shall remain distinct from its individual Occurrences.
- Changes to future recurrence shall not silently rewrite historical Occurrences.
- A missed recurring Occurrence shall not automatically become accumulating debt.
- Temporal certainty and precision shall not exceed the supporting evidence.
