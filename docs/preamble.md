# Preamble

## Parallel You

A person possesses only one body, one span of attention, and a finite amount of time and energy.

Nevertheless, a life must be lived across many concerns at once. Work, learning, relationships, health, maintenance, creativity, rest, and unexpected events all compete for the same limited capacity. Each may be important. None may act as though the others do not exist.

Parallel You exists to help a person govern that capacity.

It provides the planning, continuity, and meticulous attention that a person may need without requiring them to become meticulous merely to use it. The person lives the life. Parallel You maintains the operational model: remembering intentions, observing outcomes, proposing plans, and revising those plans when reality inevitably intervenes.

## Purpose

Parallel You helps a person:

- capture intentions before they are forgotten;
- understand where time and energy are being spent;
- form realistic plans from incomplete and changing information;
- select useful activity according to present capacity;
- preserve continuity across interruption and distraction;
- learn from actual experience;
- and reconsider, defer, reduce, delegate, or abandon commitments deliberately.

Its purpose is not to maximize activity.

Its purpose is to help the person direct finite capacity toward a life they recognize as their own.

## The Person and the Model

The person is not the model maintained by Parallel You.

Plans, estimates, classifications, observations, and histories are representations created for a purpose. They may be incomplete, uncertain, outdated, or wrong. The system shall therefore treat its model as revisable evidence rather than objective truth.

The person remains the authority concerning their intentions, values, limits, and circumstances. Parallel You may remember, infer, recommend, and reorganize. It shall not confuse assistance with sovereignty.

A recommendation is not a command.

A plan is not a promise.

An estimate is not a judgment.

A record is not a verdict.

## Time, Energy, and Attention

Available time does not imply available capacity.

A person may possess time while lacking the physical, cognitive, or emotional energy required for a particular activity. They may possess energy while lacking sustained attention. They may possess both while circumstances prevent meaningful action.

Parallel You shall represent time, energy, and attention as related but distinct conditions.

It shall not assume that every unoccupied interval is available for work, that all forms of work are interchangeable, or that human capacity can be scheduled without allowance for rest, transition, uncertainty, and interruption.

Rest is a legitimate use of time.

Recovery is not failed productivity.

Unallocated capacity is not waste.

## Planning

Planning is the construction of a useful hypothesis about the future.

Every plan is made with incomplete knowledge. New information, changing energy, unexpected obligations, inaccurate estimates, and altered priorities are ordinary conditions rather than exceptional failures.

Parallel You shall therefore support continuous revision.

When a plan becomes unrealistic, the system should help repair it. It should identify what changed, preserve what remains important, and offer practical alternatives. It shall not preserve an obsolete plan merely to measure the person against it.

The quality of a plan is determined by its usefulness under real conditions, not by the obedience it extracts.

## Observation and Learning

Parallel You may compare intention with action in order to improve future assistance.

This comparison shall be descriptive rather than punitive. A difference between what was planned and what occurred is information about the plan, the activity, the environment, or the person’s available capacity. It is not, by itself, evidence of laziness, failure, or insufficient discipline.

Repeated postponement may indicate that an activity is too large, poorly understood, incorrectly prioritized, insufficiently supported, or no longer wanted. The system should help distinguish among these possibilities.

History exists to improve understanding and continuity. It shall not become an accumulating instrument of guilt.

## Burden of Use

Parallel You must reduce the burden of personal administration.

Capturing an intention should be easier than retaining it mentally. Recording activity should require less effort than reconstructing it later. Recommendations should reduce the number of decisions demanded from the person.

Information should be inferred when it can be inferred safely, requested when it is materially useful, and omitted when its collection would cost more than its value.

Every required field, classification, reminder, and confirmation imposes work upon the person. The system shall justify that work through corresponding benefit.

A meticulous system that requires a meticulous user has failed its purpose.

## Autonomy

Parallel You acts on behalf of the person, but not in place of them.

The system may:

- organize recorded intentions;
- identify conflicts and constraints;
- propose activities and schedules;
- revise projections;
- surface neglected commitments;
- and automate reversible administrative work.

It shall preserve meaningful human choice wherever an action affects relationships, obligations, identity, safety, or other consequential interests.

Automation must remain visible, bounded, and correctable.

The person must be able to understand why a recommendation was made, reject it without penalty, and alter the assumptions from which it arose.

## Dignity and Privacy

A person’s time, energy, attention, habits, health, relationships, and intentions form an intimate record of their life.

Parallel You shall collect only what is needed to serve the person. It shall preserve the provenance and uncertainty of inferred information, distinguish observation from interpretation, and avoid presenting speculation as fact.

The person shall retain practical control over their information, including the ability to inspect, correct, export, and remove it.

The system shall not employ shame, artificial urgency, coercive engagement, or behavioural manipulation to increase use.

Success is not measured by how often the person attends to Parallel You. Success is measured by how well Parallel You enables the person to attend to their life.

## Governing Principles

Every implementation of Parallel You shall preserve the following principles:

- The person is the authority; the system is the delegate.
- The system serves the person rather than demanding service from them.
- Time, energy, and attention are finite and distinct.
- Plans are revisable hypotheses.
- Actual behaviour is evidence, not judgment.
- Rest, recovery, and unallocated capacity are legitimate.
- Abandoning an intention may be a valid and deliberate outcome.
- Recommendations remain explainable, optional, and correctable.
- Automation remains bounded and reversible wherever practicable.
- Historical information exists to improve future assistance, not to manufacture guilt.
- The cost of maintaining the system must remain lower than the value it provides.
- The ultimate purpose of planning is not productivity, but agency.
